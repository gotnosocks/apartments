How it works

Reading a gap

For every apartment on the market the model estimates the rent an ad like it usually asks, with a range around a middle. A gap is an ask below that middle. A gap can be a deal, and it can also come from an error in the ad's data or from something the ad shows and the model can't read: condition, layout, light, noise.

With a gap in hand:

  1. Read the ad on StreetEasy, photos and floor plan included.
  2. Compare the ask with the building's other asks, in the Evidence section of the ad's page here. An ask close to the neighbors' asks is ordinary for the building, whatever the model's middle says.
  3. Check the chips on the row. Each one is a reason to doubt the gap, and each is listed below.

The cautious saving

The Saving column of Deals counts dollars per month below the low end of the model's 80% range, the rent 9 in 10 similar ads ask more than. An apartment asking $3,000 with an 80% range of $3,300 to $3,900 shows $300/mo. Measured from the middle of $3,600 the gap is $600, and the cautious figure is smaller because it leaves room for the model's own error. A wide range means an unsure estimate and a small or negative saving. Deals orders rows by this saving as a share of the low end.

The range bar

Each row of Deals draws the model's ranges as nested bands: the 50%, 80% and 95% ranges, darkest in the middle. The line is the model's middle, the dot is the ask, and the ring is the ask of the apartment's previous ad.

The Evidence chip

The chip says how well the model knows the apartment, and the number counts the earlier ads it learned from.

unit history, 3 ads
The model has seen 3 earlier ads of this same apartment. This is the strongest footing.
building, 26 ads
No earlier ad of this apartment, and 26 ads of other apartments in the building.
area only
No earlier ad of the apartment and fewer than 5 in the building, so the estimate rests on the neighborhood. Deals leaves these out unless asked.

Cautions and flags

Deals hides ads with a caution unless asked. Flags stay in the list and mark facts worth checking in the ad.

No earlier ad of the apartment and an ask more than 35% below the model's middle. A gap that large on thin evidence is more often a data error than a deal.

Tags

A tag records a reading of an ad: a real deal, an error in the data, or something the model has no term for. Deals hides ads tagged as ruled out, and the Tags page exports every tag as CSV and JSON lines for cleaning rules and feature tests.

For the statistician

The model

{{ diag.model if diag else "drift-building" }} is a Bayesian regression on the log of each ad's asking rent, net of any free months. It adds up the ad's and the building's attributes, a citywide level for the week, a level for the building and a level for the unit. The unit's level weighs a recent ad of the same apartment more than an old one, and each building has a path of its own that moves a little every quarter.

Held-out estimates

An estimate here is the model's prediction for an ad whose ask it did not learn from. The latest fit holds out the newest ad of each apartment. The market fit holds out every ad on the market on the scoring day. An apartment's earlier ads have no estimate, since the model learns from them.

Cautious saving

Deals ranks by (low end - ask) / low end, with the low end the 10th percentile of the predictive distribution of the ask.

Miss on the Workbench

The Workbench measures each held-out ad's miss: the log of the ask less the log of the model's median, divided by the spread of the prediction. A miss of -2 is an ask two spreads below the median.
