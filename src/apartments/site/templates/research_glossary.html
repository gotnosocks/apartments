Measuring a model

ELPD (expected log predictive density)
How well a model predicts asking rents it has not seen, added up over listings. For each listing, it is the log of the probability the model gave the actual ask. Higher is better. Only differences between models mean anything.
PSIS-LOO
Leave-one-out: predict each listing's ask from all the others. Refitting once per listing would take years, so Pareto-smoothed importance sampling (PSIS) estimates it from a single fit.
ΔELPD and the ±
A difference in ELPD between two models on the same listings. On the frontier and the board, PSIS-LOO ΔELPD is against the simplest baseline model; in the held-out check it is against the reference model, so the two numbers for one fit differ. The ± beside each fit is its standard error against that baseline. It does not say whether two fits differ: for that, compare them directly on the same listings (a paired difference, shown in the board's notes and in the served model's decision table), whose ± is much smaller. Two fits whose paired difference is within about two of its ± are a tie.
Reference model
The PyMC model that was served before this research began. Held-out scores are measured against it so that every fit is compared with the same yardstick on the same held-out listings.
Pareto k
How trustworthy the PSIS shortcut is for one listing. Above the threshold, 1 − 1/log10(draws) capped at 0.7 (0.67 for the usual 1,000 draws), the estimate for that listing is shaky; a few such listings are normal, many mean the score should be read with care.
Held-out check
Some listings (about 10%, from apartments listed more than once) are kept out of every fit. A fit's score on them is a genuine test, used to check that the PSIS shortcut ranks models correctly.
Unit split
A harder test: every listing of about 10% of apartments held out, so the model must predict apartments it has never seen.
Fit time
Minutes from start to finish to fit a model to the full dataset on one machine's hardware. Times only compare on the same hardware. Ben's window on the RTX 2060 graphics card: a full fit of the Chelsea + West Village data within 2 hours, and exploratory fits on a subset within 30 minutes. A fit outside the window is never served.
Other cores busy on the fit's cores
How many of the processor threads a fit runs on were, on average, kept busy by other work during the fit. A fit started with the job classes of 2026-10-03 has its own half of the processor, and lighter jobs (tests, data and site builds) run on the other half, so this counts only what still landed on the fit's half. Below 0.5 the fit's time is clean: other work barely slowed it. Fits started any other way, including every fit before then, could use every core, so for them it counts other work on the whole machine.
Elegance
How coherent a model is as a statistical model of how asks arise. A more elegant model tells one generative story whose structure mirrors the data (apartments in buildings in neighbourhoods, over time). It uses few distinct mechanisms, applied consistently, and has no overlapping or competing terms. It avoids one-off flags, hand-tuned cutoffs and patches, uses natural forms for its likelihood and priors, and needs no more structural choices than what it captures. For pairs of models that matter, two independent AI judges read both model definitions without seeing any scores. Each says which one is more elegant, or that they are about equal, and why. If they disagree, the pair counts as equal. Ties in accuracy go to the more elegant model. Every judgement is listed with its reason.
Effective parameters
How many parameters the fit effectively uses, measured from the data (the in-sample fit minus the leave-one-out fit). An objective check beside the judges: of two equally accurate models, the one with fewer effective parameters is doing the same with less. Written ploo.

Choosing a model

The frontier
The fits no other fit beats on accuracy, fit time and elegance at once: anything better on one of them is worse on another. Two designs no judge has compared count as equally elegant.
Convergence checks
Whether the sampler settled on one stable answer. R-hat compares independent runs (near 1 means they agree), the effective sample size (ESS) counts how many independent draws the answer rests on, and divergences flag places the sampler could not explore. A fit that fails them is never served.
Servable
A fit the automatic selection may serve: it passes the convergence checks, has a PSIS-LOO score, ran on the target hardware within the time window on the full dataset, and uses the current data rules. Exploration fits and subset fits are never servable.
Automatic selection
The rule that picks the served model from the servable fits: the most accurate, then among ties the most elegant, then the fastest. It replaces the served model only when another fit clearly beats it.
Exploration fit
A short fit, with fewer draws than a full fit, run to see quickly whether a design is worth a full fit. Exploration fits count on the research frontier, so the research can be judged by them, but they are never served: only a full fit's estimates reach the listings, buildings and rent map. On the charts they are diamonds; full fits are circles.
Full fit
A fit with enough draws to pass the convergence checks on the full dataset. Only full fits can be served.
Subset fit
A fit on a part of the data, used to tune settings quickly. Exploration only; never served.
Data rules
Fixed corrections applied before fitting, such as counting the same apartment's differently written labels as one, and leaving out listings a review found are not an open-market lease of a whole apartment.

Estimates

Estimate
The model's typical asking rent for that apartment, in that building, that month, made without the listing's own ask (leave-own-row-out). See how estimates work.
Below typical, typical, above typical
Where the ask falls among the asks the model expects for that apartment: the lowest 10%, the middle 80%, or the highest 10%. The middle 80% is the likely ask range shown on listing pages and in listing tables when the estimates carry it.
