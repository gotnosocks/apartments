The frontier

Every model fit on the board, compared three ways: how accurately it predicts asks it has not seen, how long it takes to fit, and how elegant it is as a statistical model. A fit is on the frontier when no other fit beats it on one of these without losing on another. The served model is chosen automatically among the fits that can be served: the most accurate, then the most elegant, then the fastest, and it changes only when another fit clearly beats it.

What these measures mean

More terms are in the glossary.

PSIS-LOO ΔELPD
How well a fit predicts each listing's asking rent from all the other listings, compared with the simplest baseline model. It is summed over the {{ "{:,}".format(meta.provenance.rows_in_fit) if meta.provenance.rows_in_fit else "" }} listings in the fit, on a log scale; higher is better. The ± is against the baseline: two fits tie when their paired difference on the same listings is within about two of its own, much smaller, ± (why).
Fit time
Minutes from start to finish to fit the model to the full dataset on one machine's hardware. Only fits on the same hardware compare.
Elegance
How coherent a model is as a statistical model of how asks arise. A more elegant model tells one generative story whose structure mirrors the data (apartments in buildings in neighbourhoods, over time). It uses few distinct mechanisms, applied consistently, and has no overlapping or competing terms. It avoids one-off flags, hand-tuned cutoffs and patches, uses natural forms for its likelihood and priors, and needs no more structural choices than what it captures. For pairs of models that matter, two independent AI judges read both model definitions without seeing any scores. Each says which one is more elegant, or that they are about equal, and why. If they disagree, the pair counts as equal. Ties in accuracy go to the more elegant model. The table counts each fit's judged pairs.
Convergence checks
Whether the sampler settled on one stable answer (R-hat and effective sample size). A fit that fails them is shown hollow and never served.
Exploration fits
Short fits (fewer draws) that show quickly whether a design is promising. They count on the frontier, drawn as diamonds, and are never served; a promising design gets a full fit.
Subset fits
Fits on a part of the data, used to tune settings quickly. They are exploration only, never served.
