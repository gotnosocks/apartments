Propose to Ben replacing the half-width null at autoselect.py:351 with max(3 x A/A SD, 2 x bootstrap SE). Back-fill C, areatime, lines, nofloorslope, latest-ask and the held-out-lots refit.

**weakness:** The new-building score has no lpd, SE, per-row output or pairing, and nothing reads it (reader finding 3), so it cannot decide anything. Coverage alone cannot judge a change in width. Paired PSIS-LOO SEs are iid over rows (loo.py:305; leaderboard.py:297, :429, :449), although rows cluster by building. So lines +45 +- 16, elevfill +33 +- 14 and bigbed +43 +- 18 rest on iid SEs. shared_rows pairs by audit_id without checking y (leaderboard.py:241-254). The less-descriptive tie-break uses a posterior half-width of about 0.7 pp, not run-to-run noise, so it cannot fire on 0.1-0.2 pp moves. Four explanations of the 90.7/98.1 over-width are untested.

**mechanism:** lpd is a proper score: it pays for a better centre (explanation) and for calibrated width, so it sees what the median miss cannot. Clustering makes buildings the unit of inference. This is the Moulton problem: variance is inflated by about 1+(m-1)rho, with m in the hundreds. Clustering removes false wins. An A/A of the posterior-mean share measures the run-to-run noise that Ben's null is meant to capture, which removes false ties. The breakdowns separate the candidate causes of over-width. Coverage that rises with calendar year points to the walk origin; with bedroom or bath, to independent slope priors; building-weighted coverage near nominal points to one building scale; a signed bias in small buildings points to the centre.
