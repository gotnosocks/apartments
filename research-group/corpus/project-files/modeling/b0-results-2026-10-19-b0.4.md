# B0.4 fit: results (did not converge)

*Fit 2026-10-19. B0.4 is B0.3 plus location. Manhattan is the baseline and Brooklyn gets an offset. Queens (24 buildings) and the 76 buildings without coordinates get their own intercepts. Manhattan (box rotated 29° to the street grid) and Brooklyn each get their own HSGP surface, with a squared-exponential kernel, the length-scale fixed at 500 m, an amplitude per borough, and non-centered coefficients. s_bath's prior was loosened to HalfNormal(0.3). The data are the trimmed rows from December 2013 on. PR #1152, merged as 065cc73e. Nothing on the served model or the site changed.*

## Run

- `b0.4-b0-latest-065cc73e-b0.4-dense-w1500`, fit-only, on an A100-40GB.
- 16 chains, 1,500 warmup steps, 500 draws (every 12th kept).
- 30.4 minutes and $1.21, against an estimate of $1.06.
- No divergences while sampling. The worst R-hat is 11.6 and the lowest ESS is 8.

## Why it doesn't converge: three scales stuck too high

In each case the scale parameter sits far above what its own coefficients imply. The coefficients shrink to compensate, and the chains don't move along that ridge.

| Scale | Fitted | What its coefficients imply |
|---|---|---|
| τ (walk) | ~0.033 | weekly shocks sd 0.0045; the log density peaks at τ ≈ 0.004 in every chain, 1,044 units above the fitted value (B0.3's gap was 703) |
| Manhattan amplitude | 11.8 | surface spread 0.18 log |
| Brooklyn amplitude | 3.8 | surface spread 0.19 log |
| s_bath | 1.26 | — |

The pooled bath means, α, the season and the drift trade off against these scales, and they fail R-hat. What the data pin down agrees across chains to within 1–2%: every ladder cell, both surfaces and the residual scale. So this is a structure problem (non-centered scales on 1.9M rows), not a data problem.

## What location changed, B0.3 → B0.4 (unconverged, but cell-level quantities agree across chains)

**One bath above the usual count:**

| Beds | B0.3 | B0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | +95% | +74% |
| 1 | +50% | +37% |
| 2 | +71% | +41% |
| 3 | +45% | +28% |
| 4 | +73% | +51% |
| 5+ | +43% | +27% |

**Bedroom steps along the usual-bath path:**

| Step | B0.3 | B0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Studio → 1 bed | +21% | +28% |
| 1 → 2 bed | +1.1% | +14.9% |
| 2 → 3 bed | +8% | +17% |
| 3 → 4 bed | +46% | +39% |

**Other changes:**
- **Noise.** The residual scale fell from 0.24–0.34 to 0.17–0.19. The tails got heavier: the t degrees of freedom fell from 21 to 6.5.
- **Borough offsets.** Brooklyn sits at −24.5% against Manhattan, and Queens at −7%.
- **Trend.** With location in, the trend runs about 8% steeper from 2014 to 2026. It is 7% lower at the start and 5% higher at the end of 2021, which fits a listing mix that drifted toward cheaper places.

## Surfaces

The map is at `/data1/apartments/frontier/maps/b0.4/b04_surfaces.png` on thelio. The data reach about 96th–110th Street.
- **Manhattan.** Hot in Tribeca, the West Village, West Chelsea and along Fifth Avenue on the Upper East Side. Cold in East Harlem and the Lower East Side.
- **Brooklyn.** Hot in Williamsburg, Brooklyn Heights, DUMBO and Park Slope. It falls off steadily toward Crown Heights and Brownsville.

The surfaces follow neighbourhoods rather than single buildings, so the 500 m scale looks right.

## Predictions: 3 hits, 4 misses

**Hits:**
- The 1→2 bed step rose above +5%.
- The trend moved by more than 3%.
- The Brooklyn offset came in at −0.28, inside the predicted −0.45 to −0.10.

**Misses:**
- All the bath steps fell, but three landed outside +30% to +65%.
- s_bath rose to 1.26 instead of falling below 0.45.
- The amplitude parameters came out at 11.8 and 3.8, although the surface spreads (0.18, 0.19) sit inside the predicted ranges.
- The fit did not converge.

## Proposed next step (not started)

1. **Center the scales.** Use a centered walk, centered surface coefficients and centered pooled bath steps, so that each scale is set directly by its coefficients.
2. **Starting the scales near their implied values is the weaker option.** In B0.3, τ climbed away from 0.002 during warmup anyway.
