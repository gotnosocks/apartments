# B0.3 fit on trimmed data: results (did not converge)

*Fit 2026-10-19. B0.3 is B0.2 with bed and bath steps re-anchored on the typical bath count for each bedroom level, and each bath step pooled across bedroom levels by its position (−1, +1, +2, +3). It ran on the trimmed dataset: the trend starts 2013-12-02, and the 18 earlier rows are set aside (PR #1151). This was a fit-only run, with no LOO or held-out scoring. Nothing on the served model or the site changed.*

## Run

- `b0.3-b0-latest-ce3f6da4-b0.3-dense-w1500` on an A100-40GB.
- 16 chains, 1,500 warmup, 500 draws (every 12th kept).
- 26.8 minutes and $1.06, against an estimate of $0.87.
- No divergences while drawing, 618 in warmup. Acceptance 0.88, with 32.5 leapfrog steps on average.

## What the trim fixed

- **Trend level:** the chain medians now agree within 0.033 log across the whole span. In B0.2 they were up to 0.37 apart before 2014.
- **Intercept:** chain medians span 7.805 to 7.831, against 7.73 to 8.05 in B0.2. Its R-hat fell from 5.9 to 2.2.
- **Still mixing poorly:** the level R-hats are still a median 1.9, because each chain's own band is narrower than the spread between chains.

## Reading

- **The trim worked.** The 18 early rows were what let the chains disagree on the level and the intercept.
- **The cells barely moved from B0.2,** as expected: 24 cells with plenty of ads each.
- **Pooling is the new sticking point.** Each pool averages only 2 to 6 precisely measured steps that differ by far more than their intervals. So s_bath sits at 0.6, and the pooled means wander between chains. The same-building check says those differences are mostly location, so a location term should shrink them.
- **Drift and season are still unconverged,** and the trim didn't touch them. They are next to investigate, starting with the data.
