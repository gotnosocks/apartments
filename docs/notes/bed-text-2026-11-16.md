# Ads coded with a bedroom more than their text describes

Ben asked on Nov 16 for a win from the data problems in the tails of L2's errors. Reading
L2's worst held-out misses on the low side, the ads often described a smaller unit than the
coded `bedrooms`: a "flex 2" or "convertible 3" coded at the flexed count, a "junior 1" coded
as a one-bedroom, a "studio duplex" coded as a one-bedroom, a "1 bed + home office" coded as
a two-bedroom. L2 takes size from the coded count alone, so it prices these as full units of
that count.

## The columns

`rentfrontier.b0bedtext` reads the description of each listing's latest record and writes
four columns into a copy of L2's dataset. Its docstring gives the passes in full. The columns
come from the text and the coded bedroom count, and no rent enters them.

- `bed_flex_at`: flex wording for a count equal to the coded one.
- `bed_text_fewer`: every size the text states for the unit is below the coded count.
- `bed_text_more`: every stated size is above the coded count, with no flex wording.
- `bed_text`: the stated size nearest the coded count, NaN where the text states none or
  lists sizes of several units.

I built the copy from L2's dataset (rows SHA-256
`d901a565b7836cea82aac6924e8df5139271aa1292508811dd121fabaa0c7a3d`) under a 3 GB cap:

    D=/data1/apartments/frontier/datasets/b0-b3ddebf19af1dc1e-alldata-v36-dense-nyc-closingraise
    python -m rentfrontier.b0bedtext $D $D-bedtext

The copy's rows SHA-256 is `ad7dc8dd33d468e76f90d0ae8bb34f67ace8bca30f6913c8d85454ae2877eb3d`.
Of its 1,896,471 rows, 972,211 get a `bed_text`, 40,922 `bed_flex_at`, 18,312
`bed_text_fewer`, 8,977 `bed_text_more`, and 56,650 either of the first two.

## Screen against L2's held-out predictions

`bed-text-2026-11-16/screen.py` joins the columns to the scores of L2's pool
(`drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-b0-latest-b27d1dd8-l2kn`) on the latest split, leaving
out income-restricted ads. z is the ask less L2's median, over the spread implied by L2's
quartiles.

| Held-out ads | Ads | Above L2's 99th pct | Below L2's 1st pct | Mean z | Mean log miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bed_flex_at` | 3,637 | 0.77% | 2.20% | -0.342 | -0.0226 |
| `bed_text_fewer` | 1,578 | 0.70% | 3.11% | -0.462 | -0.0311 |
| either ("smaller") | 5,019 | 0.76% | 2.53% | -0.375 | -0.0250 |
| `bed_text_more` | 608 | 5.10% | 0.49% | +0.451 | +0.0321 |
| all | 189,440 | 0.94% | 1.14% | -0.071 | -0.0043 |

The patterns were written while reading misses in half of the buildings. In the other half,
whose ads nobody read, the 2,577 smaller ads have mean z -0.389 and 2.87% below the 1st
percentile, and the 291 `bed_text_more` ads have mean z +0.487. So the patterns were not
fitted to the ads they are scored on.

By coded count, the smaller ads' mean z is -0.442 at 1 bedroom (1,841 ads), -0.359 at 2
(1,914), -0.299 at 3 (946), -0.298 at 4 (276) and -0.401 at 5 (42).

The shift is about 2% of rent beyond the 0.4% all held-out ads share. A flex 2 priced as a
full 2 is off by less than the gap between a 1 and a 2, since L2's unit level and unit means
already carry most of a relisted unit's discount.

The flags matter for the Deals list more than their elpd suggests. With the scratch version
of the patterns, 31 of 446 uncautioned candidates among 6,774 open ads carried the smaller
flag, 7% against 2.6% of held-out ads, and about 29 of the 31 read as real cases. A unit
coded a bedroom too high looks cheap for its coded size.

Known misreads: "spacious one-bedroom ... king-sized bed" in an ad of a two-bedroom reads as
a stated size of 1. Amenity studios ("wellbeats studio", "yoga studio") are skipped.

## The fit

`drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedtext` is L2 with the ad column `ad:bed_text_smaller`
(either flag) and its unit mean, both under the flag prior. It is the only change from L2,
on L2's rows and split. `bed_text_more` stays out of this fit, as a second change with a
tenth of the rows.

Three H100 containers, seeds 1 to 3, from the merge commit of this note's PR, pooled into 6
chains, as L2's were:

```sh
D=/data1/apartments/frontier/datasets/b0-b3ddebf19af1dc1e-alldata-v36-dense-nyc-closingraise-bedtext
IN=/data1/apartments/pipeline/datasets/rows-b3ddebf19af1dc1e.parquet
ops/modal-fit --outside-budget --gpu H100 --dataset "$D" --input "$IN" --split latest \
  --baseline drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-b0-latest-b27d1dd8-l2kn COMMIT l2bt-a \
  drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedtext b0 2 500 9000 40 --seed 1 --level-dof 4 \
  --no-loo --no-heldout
```

I score the pool against L2's with `python -m rentfrontier.drift_score`. L2's containers
settled at $11.06.

## Predictions (written before the fit)

1. The pool passes the gate (80%). L2's own latest-split pool passed, and the new columns
   are 2 among about 900 global ones.
2. The coefficient of `ad:bed_text_smaller` has a posterior mean between -0.035 and -0.012
   (70%) and its 95% interval lies below 0 (95%).
3. The unit mean's coefficient has a posterior mean within 0.02 of 0 (55%).
4. The pool leads L2 on held-out elpd by 100 to 350 (65%), and by more than 2 paired SE
   (80%).
5. On the smaller held-out ads, mean z moves from -0.375 to between -0.15 and 0 (70%), and
   the share below the 1st percentile falls from 2.53% to under 1.8% (65%).
6. The overall median absolute error moves by under 0.0001 (85%).
7. The three containers settle between $10.50 and $12.00 in all (75%).

## If it passes

The term then goes into the market and backtest fits of whichever model next serves Deals,
since those fits read the same dataset copy. Until then the flag can mark Deals candidates
with a caution, at no fit cost.
