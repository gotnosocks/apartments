# The drift model: a unit level that moves over time (2026-11-10)

Ben asked on 2026-11-10 for a descriptive model of asking rents that helps find good deals.
A deal is an ask well under what the model expects for that apartment, so the model has to
predict a unit's next ask as closely as the data allow. This note records a free check that
set the design, the model, and the predictions written before its first fit.

The module is `rentfrontier.drift`. Its docstring holds the model and the sampler.

## The model

The kitchen sink's columns, surface, building levels, walk and noise
(`rentfrontier.building_zones_kitchen_sink`), plus for each unit a lasting level s and an
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift d:

    u_i = s_unit(i) + d_unit(i)(t_i),  s ~ Normal(0, ss^2),
    Var d = sd^2,  Corr(d(t), d(t')) = exp(-|t - t'| / tau)

Fit 4 failed the convergence gate on 68 of 121 coefficients, because its sweep drew the
coefficients with the 935k unit effects held fixed. The drift sampler integrates the unit
term out of the draw of the coefficients, walk and surface, out of the building levels, and
out of the draw of (ss, sd, tau). The tests in `frontier/tests/test_drift.py` check each of
these against the dense Gaussian algebra on small designs.

## Predictions for the first fit

The first fit runs on the dataset, split and held-out ads of fits 4, 8 and kitchen sink (a):
`b0-b3ddebf19af1dc1e-alldata-v36-dense-nyc`, latest split, 189,647 held-out ads. Reference
scores on those ads: fit 8 elpd 148,234 (RMSE 0.1158), kitchen sink (a) 156,105 (0.1112),
fit 4 177,137 (0.1018).

1. It passes the gate (max R-hat at most 1.01, min ESS over 400), the bedroom, bath and floor
   columns included. I put this at 65%. The likeliest miss is one of the three unit scales.
2. ss comes out at 0.04 to 0.065, sd at 0.07 to 0.10 and tau at 2 to 4.5 years.
3. sigma falls to 0.045 to 0.055 in the two smallest bedroom groups, from the kitchen
   sink's 0.081 and 0.079. nu stays between 3.5 and 5.
4. Held-out elpd is 183,000 to 195,000, which is 6,000 to 18,000 over fit 4, and RMSE of the
   predictive median is 0.093 to 0.098.
5. The 90% intervals cover 0.89 to 0.915 of the held-out ads.
6. The gain over fit 4 is largest for ads within a year of the unit's last training ad and
   under 1,000 elpd in total for ads 5 years or more after it.
7. `ad:months_free` stays within 1 point of the kitchen sink's -5.68.
8. A sweep of 4 chains takes at most 2.5 s on an A100, so the fit stays under the hour cap.

## Second free check: what fit 4's residuals still share

Fit 4 has a constant level for each unit, so its residuals show what is left once units are
accounted for. On its training residuals (variance 0.0070):

- Two ads of different units of one building correlate at 0.26 when they ran within three
  months of each other. The correlation falls through 0.13, 0.075 and 0.016 over the next
  bins of time apart, and is slightly negative beyond. A building-by-time term holds at
  most about 20% of the residual variance, with a correlation time near 0.4 to 0.5 years.
- Neighbourhood-by-year means hold about 2.5% of the variance, most of it in 2025 and 2026
  (West Village 2026 at +0.041, East Harlem 2026 at -0.030).
- Zone-by-time means hold about 1.3% and bedroom-by-time means about 1%.

So a landlord moves a whole building's asks together for a few months, and this is the
largest structure left. Neighbourhood moves are small on average and concentrated in the
most recent years, which are the ones a deal score reads.

## Local drift: one process per building, then one per neighbourhood

`rentfrontier.drift_building` adds to the drift model a stationary AR(1) process for each
building in monthly steps, with sd s_b ~ HalfNormal(0.1) and step correlation rho_b ~
Uniform(0, 1). `rentfrontier.drift_local` adds a second one for each neighbourhood in
quarterly steps. Each is one change to the model and gets its own fit on the latest split.
The sampler draws each term's two scales with its paths integrated out (a Kalman filter over
the steps), then the paths (`rentfrontier.drift`'s docstring).

### Predictions for `drift-building` (written before its fit, and before the drift fit's scores)

1. It passes the gate. I put this at 50%: the building term and the units' drift both explain
   a unit's recent ads in a building with few units, and the two sds can trade off.
2. The building sd is 0.025 to 0.045 and the monthly rho is 0.80 to 0.93, a correlation time
   of 0.4 to 1.2 years.
3. The units' drift sd falls by 0.005 to 0.02 from the drift fit's, and sigma in the two
   smallest bedroom groups falls by a further 0.002 to 0.006.
4. Held-out elpd is 2,500 to 9,000 over the drift fit's. The gain per ad is largest for
   held-out ads whose unit last ran two or more years earlier while another unit of the
   building ran within six months.
5. The 90% intervals cover 0.89 to 0.915 of the held-out ads.
6. A sweep takes at most 0.5 s longer than the drift fit's.

### Predictions for `drift-local` (written before its fit)

1. It passes the gate at 50%, given that `drift-building` does.
2. The neighbourhood sd is 0.008 to 0.02 and the quarterly rho is 0.85 to 0.98.
3. Held-out elpd is 300 to 2,500 over `drift-building`'s. The latest split holds out a
   unit's last ad, so its held-out ads lean recent, and the neighbourhood moves are largest
   in 2025 and 2026.
4. The building term's sd and rho move by less than their posterior sds.

The model with the highest held-out elpd among those that pass the gate is fitted on the
fresh data and serves the deal scores.
