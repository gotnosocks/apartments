# The drift model: a unit level that moves over time (2026-11-10)

Ben asked on 2026-11-10 for a descriptive model of asking rents that helps find good deals.
A deal is an ask well under what the model expects for that apartment, so the model has to
predict a unit's next ask as closely as the data allow. This note records a free check that
set the design, the model, and the predictions written before its first fit.

The module is `rentfrontier.drift`. Its docstring holds the model and the sampler.

## The model

The kitchen sink's columns, surface, building levels, walk and noise
(`rentfrontier.building_zones_kitchen_sink`), plus for each unit a lasting level s and an
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift d:

    u_i = s_unit(i) + d_unit(i)(t_i),  s ~ Normal(0, ss^2),
    Var d = sd^2,  Corr(d(t), d(t')) = exp(-|t - t'| / tau)

Fit 4 failed the convergence gate on 68 of 121 coefficients, because its sweep drew the
coefficients with the 935k unit effects held fixed. The drift sampler integrates the unit
term out of the draw of the coefficients, walk and surface, out of the building levels, and
out of the draw of (ss, sd, tau). The tests in `frontier/tests/test_drift.py` check each of
these against the dense Gaussian algebra on small designs.

## Local drift: one process per building, then one per neighbourhood

`rentfrontier.drift_building` adds to the drift model a stationary AR(1) process for each
building in monthly steps, with sd s_b ~ HalfNormal(0.1) and step correlation rho_b ~
Uniform(0, 1). `rentfrontier.drift_local` adds a second one for each neighbourhood in
quarterly steps. Each is one change to the model and gets its own fit on the latest split.
The sampler draws each term's two scales with its paths integrated out (a Kalman filter over
the steps), then the paths (`rentfrontier.drift`'s docstring).

1. It passes the gate. I put this at 50%: the building term and the units' drift both explain
   a unit's recent ads in a building with few units, and the two sds can trade off.
2. The building sd is 0.025 to 0.045 and the monthly rho is 0.80 to 0.93, a correlation time
   of 0.4 to 1.2 years.
3. The units' drift sd falls by 0.005 to 0.02 from the drift fit's, and sigma in the two
   smallest bedroom groups falls by a further 0.002 to 0.006.
4. Held-out elpd is 2,500 to 9,000 over the drift fit's. The gain per ad is largest for
   held-out ads whose unit last ran two or more years earlier while another unit of the
   building ran within six months.
5. The 90% intervals cover 0.89 to 0.915 of the held-out ads.
6. A sweep takes at most 0.5 s longer than the drift fit's.

Following the rule for a fit that misses the gate, nothing was rerun on another seed. No row
of any dataset lacks a neighbourhood, so the second review's finding on that changed no fit.

**`drift` misses narrowly.** The ten quantities are the four sigmas, nu, the three unit
scales and two studio coefficients. Pooled over chains: unit_sd 0.0483, drift_sd 0.0786,
drift_tau 5.56 years, nu 1.756, sigma 0.0225 to 0.0353. The sweep draws sigma given the unit
levels u, and u given sigma. With u given, 1.7 million residuals pin sigma tightly. With u
integrated out only the repeat ads of a unit inform it, so its posterior is much wider than
the step each sweep can take. The unit scales were already drawn with u integrated out, but
given sigma.

The round-two traces (1,000 sweeps a chain) fit that reading in part. Within a chain the
sigmas have the longest autocorrelation times, 20 to 84 sweeps, then nu at 23 to 46, then
the unit scales at 14 to 32. The three unit scales lie on a ridge (correlations of their
logs: unit_sd and drift_sd -0.96, unit_sd and drift_tau -0.93). The sigmas correlate only
weakly with drift_sd (+0.14 to +0.28) and more with nu (+0.59 to +0.69). So the trade-off is
between sigma and the unit levels themselves, not between sigma and the drift's scale, and
nu follows sigma.

**The building term misses badly.** building_ar_sd comes out at 0.074 and its monthly rho at
0.943 (a half-life near a year), both far from what I predicted (0.025 to 0.045, 0.80 to
0.93). With the term in the model the units' drift_sd falls from 0.079 to 0.051 and its time
scale rises from 5.6 to about 14 years: the building path takes over most of what moved the
units together. The chains agree on the means to three digits and still fail, because each
chain creeps. The path is drawn given u, u given the path, and the building level given the
path, and each of those conditionals is far tighter than the posterior.

## Round four: the building term with a collapsed step (predictions written before launch)

Written at 04:20Z on 2026-11-10, while round three runs and before the first twelve fits'
held-out scores are in.

The building term failed because its path was drawn given u. The new step (`update_cells`
in `drift.py`) treats a building's level and its path at the steps where it has rows as one
Gaussian state, and draws it with u integrated out: the rows of a building given that state
have precision B' W B, with W the units' precisions that the sweep already builds. The
zones' spreads and the path's (log s, logit rho) are slice sampled on the same system's
marginal likelihood, with the levels, the paths and u all integrated out, along directions
set in the warmup. The step is checked against dense algebra in `test_drift_local.py` (the
marginal likelihood, each building's conditional mean and covariance, and the fill of the
steps between cells).

Two things change with it, both forced by the dense factors: the building's steps are
quarters, not months (at most 52 cells a building in place of 155; round two's monthly rho
of 0.94 is 0.84 a quarter, so little is lost), and the neighbourhood term is left out
(`drift-local` keeps its uncollapsed step and is not refit).

What stays uncollapsed: the coefficients, the walk and the surface are still drawn given
the paths, and the unit scales and sigma given the paths. Those are the places a miss would
come from.

A pilot comes first (latest split, 200 warmup, 300 draws) to time the sweep and read the
mixing, then the three full fits (600 warmup, 1,000 draws) if the pilot's sweep is under
3 s and its least ESS is on pace for the gate.

1. A sweep takes under 3.0 s (round two: 1.2 s). 60%.
2. building_ar_sd is between 0.060 and 0.085, and the quarterly rho between 0.78 and 0.90.
3. The full latest-split fit's worst R-hat is under 1.05 (round two: 1.65). 70%.
4. It passes the gate. 35%. A miss is on drift_tau or unit_sd (their ridge is longer with
   the building term: tau near 14 years in round two), or on walk levels, not on the
   building term's own scales.
5. Its held-out elpd is more than 4,000 above the plain drift model's on the same 189,647
   ads. 70%.
6. With u integrated out the path's scales have wider posteriors than round two's traces
   showed: the sd of log building_ar_sd within a chain is over 0.01 (round one: 0.005 to
   0.007).

## Round five: every scale drawn with every level integrated out (predictions written before launch)

Written at 05:07Z on 2026-11-10.

Three rounds have now failed the same way one level apart: a scale is drawn given a latent
that pins it. Sigma given u (fixed in round three), the unit scales given the buildings'
paths (the pilot), the unit scales and sigma given the buildings' levels and nu given u
(what round three still misses on). Round five removes the pattern in one step,
`update_scales`:

- The unit scales (ss, sd, tau), the four sigmas and nu are slice sampled together along
  eight directions set in the warmup, with u, the buildings' levels and the buildings'
  paths all integrated out. The density is the units' Kalman likelihood plus each
  building's term from B' W B, both rebuilt at every point tried.
- nu has no closed form with the mixing scales lam integrated out, so lam rides along:
  each row keeps its standardized log lam while nu moves (log lam = m(nu) + s(nu) eta,
  with m and s the mean and sd of log lam under nu). The map's Jacobian and lam's density
  are in the target, so the step is exact. The noise step still draws nu, sigma and lam
  given u afterwards.
- The step comes right after theta, and the levels, the paths and u are drawn again at
  the new scales before anything reads them.
- `scales_logp` is checked against the dense density with every scale moving, for both
  models (`test_drift.py`, `test_drift_local.py`).

Two smaller changes ride with it, since every fit so far has failed and none of these
comparisons is lost:

- **Two bath steps are folded** (`drift.DROPPED_STEPS`). `bed=0:full>=3` is set by 37
  training ads of 34 units. Their median rent is $6,095 against a studio's $2,500: they
  read like larger apartments entered with no bedrooms, and the step drew a premium of
  52% (0.42 with sd 0.043). It was the worst quantity in round three's fits (R-hat 1.027,
  ESS 188). `bed=1:full>=4` has 26 ads. The project already folds `bed=2:full>=4` (46 ads)
  for the same reason. No ad is recoded: a studio with three baths now takes the premium of
  one with two.
- **A leaner slice** (`slice_along`): the log density of each accepted point is carried to
  the next direction, which saves an evaluation a direction.

Modal stops a fit's container at 75 minutes and the project's rule is an hour a fit, so the
lengths are set to fit: `drift` 500 warmup and 1,500 draws, `drift-building` 400 and 1,000,
on an H100 if a sweep is over 2 s.

1. A `drift` sweep takes 1.2 to 1.8 s (0.91 s in round three). A `drift-building` sweep
   takes 2.0 to 3.2 s on an A100 (1.50 s in the pilot).
2. In `drift`, the least ESS among the unit scales, the sigmas and nu, per 1,000 draws of
   4 chains, rises from 188 to over 1,000. 70%.
3. `drift` on the latest split passes the gate. 65%. A miss is on a coefficient with few
   rows behind it, not on a scale.
4. In `drift-building`, unit_sd, drift_sd and drift_tau have R-hat under 1.02 (the pilot:
   1.7 to 1.8). 75%.
5. `drift-building` on the latest split passes the gate. 40%. A miss is on a coefficient
   of a building-level column or on the walk, drawn given the paths.
6. Posterior means move little: `drift`'s scales within 2 pooled sd of round three's, and
   `drift-building`'s unit_sd between 0.055 and 0.062 with drift_tau between 8 and 12.
7. Held-out elpd: `drift` within 150 of 195,315 (the folded steps touch 60 held-out ads at
   most). `drift-building` between 229,500 and 232,500.

## Round six: slice directions from the scale step's own curvature, and the building columns drawn free of the paths (predictions written before launch)

Written at 07:02Z on 2026-11-10.

The scale step draws eight scales given theta and the rows' standardized mixing scales, one
slice along each of eight directions. The directions come from the covariance of the
scales' warmup draws, so they are conjugate for the posterior. But the step does not sample
the posterior. It samples the scales given the standardized mixing scales, and that density
has another shape: with those held fixed, nu and the four sigmas can move a long way
together (a higher nu raises every lam, and higher sigmas undo it) and hardly at all apart.
Directions cut for the posterior cross that ridge at an angle, each slice is short, and one
round of eight moves the scales along the ridge by a fraction of what the density allows.
The unit scales' conditional mean depends on where sigma and nu sit, so they are dragged
along at the ridge's pace.

Round three's step had no such ridge, because nu stayed out of it. That fits the numbers:
round five's density conditions on less than round three's, so with exact draws from it
the unit scales could not have slowed.

The change (`scales_curvature`, `conditional_dirs`): at each of the two points in the
warmup where the directions are set, the Hessian of the step's log density is measured at
each chain's state by finite differences (73 evaluations), averaged over chains, and the
directions become four times a square root of its negative inverse. Nothing else in the
sweep changes, and the directions stay fixed after the warmup. The log prints the
eigenvalues of the step's covariance over the window's, which is the share of the
posterior's variance that one scale update can renew along each axis: a direct measure of
how much the mixing scales and theta pin the scales.

A second change, for the building model only (`update_slow`). In p5b ten coefficients of
building amenity codes had autocorrelation times of 11 to 24 sweeps (lag-one
autocorrelation 0.75 to 0.91). The same ten are at 1.4 to 2.7 sweeps in every fit without
the collapsed building term (f3, p5d and round three's market fit) and at 9 to 24 in the
pilot of round four, so the collapsed term brought it. An amenity code is set from the
building's ads up to each row's day, so it changes within a building over time, and the
building's path can stand in for it. theta is drawn given the paths, which pins these
coefficients. The new step draws the 50 or so `bld:` coefficients once more each sweep with
u, the levels and the paths all integrated out, right before the paths are drawn. It is
checked against the dense regression (`test_drift_local.py`). `drift` has no such term and
does not run the step, so its pilot isolates the directions.

Checkpoints are off, as above.

Two pilots like round five's (4 chains, 200 warmup, 300 draws, A100), so the comparison is
like for like:
