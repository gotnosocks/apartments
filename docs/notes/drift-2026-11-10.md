# The drift model: a unit level that moves over time (2026-11-10)

Ben asked on 2026-11-10 for a descriptive model of asking rents that helps find good deals.
A deal is an ask well under what the model expects for that apartment, so the model has to
predict a unit's next ask as closely as the data allow. This note records a free check that
set the design, the model, and the predictions written before its first fit.

The module is `rentfrontier.drift`. Its docstring holds the model and the sampler.

## The model

The kitchen sink's columns, surface, building levels, walk and noise
(`rentfrontier.building_zones_kitchen_sink`), plus for each unit a lasting level s and an
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift d:

    u_i = s_unit(i) + d_unit(i)(t_i),  s ~ Normal(0, ss^2),
    Var d = sd^2,  Corr(d(t), d(t')) = exp(-|t - t'| / tau)

Fit 4 failed the convergence gate on 68 of 121 coefficients, because its sweep drew the
coefficients with the 935k unit effects held fixed. The drift sampler integrates the unit
term out of the draw of the coefficients, walk and surface, out of the building levels, and
out of the draw of (ss, sd, tau). The tests in `frontier/tests/test_drift.py` check each of
these against the dense Gaussian algebra on small designs.

## Predictions for the first fit

`b0-b3ddebf19af1dc1e-alldata-v36-dense-nyc`, latest split, 189,647 held-out ads. Reference
scores on those ads: fit 8 elpd 148,234 (RMSE 0.1158), kitchen sink (a) 156,105 (0.1112),
fit 4 177,137 (0.1018).

1. It passes the gate (max R-hat at most 1.01, min ESS over 400), the bedroom, bath and floor
   columns included. I put this at 65%. The likeliest miss is one of the three unit scales.
2. ss comes out at 0.04 to 0.065, sd at 0.07 to 0.10 and tau at 2 to 4.5 years.
3. sigma falls to 0.045 to 0.055 in the two smallest bedroom groups, from the kitchen
   sink's 0.081 and 0.079. nu stays between 3.5 and 5.
4. Held-out elpd is 183,000 to 195,000, which is 6,000 to 18,000 over fit 4, and RMSE of the
   predictive median is 0.093 to 0.098.
5. The 90% intervals cover 0.89 to 0.915 of the held-out ads.
6. The gain over fit 4 is largest for ads within a year of the unit's last training ad and
   under 1,000 elpd in total for ads 5 years or more after it.
7. `ad:months_free` stays within 1 point of the kitchen sink's -5.68.
8. A sweep of 4 chains takes at most 2.5 s on an A100, so the fit stays under the hour cap.

## Second free check: what fit 4's residuals still share

Fit 4 has a constant level for each unit, so its residuals show what is left once units are
accounted for. On its training residuals (variance 0.0070):

- Two ads of different units of one building correlate at 0.26 when they ran within three
  months of each other. The correlation falls through 0.13, 0.075 and 0.016 over the next
  bins of time apart, and is slightly negative beyond. A building-by-time term holds at
  most about 20% of the residual variance, with a correlation time near 0.4 to 0.5 years.
- Neighbourhood-by-year means hold about 2.5% of the variance, most of it in 2025 and 2026
  (West Village 2026 at +0.041, East Harlem 2026 at -0.030).
- Zone-by-time means hold about 1.3% and bedroom-by-time means about 1%.

So a landlord moves a whole building's asks together for a few months, and this is the
largest structure left. Neighbourhood moves are small on average and concentrated in the
most recent years, which are the ones a deal score reads.

## Local drift: one process per building, then one per neighbourhood

`rentfrontier.drift_building` adds to the drift model a stationary AR(1) process for each
building in monthly steps, with sd s_b ~ HalfNormal(0.1) and step correlation rho_b ~
Uniform(0, 1). `rentfrontier.drift_local` adds a second one for each neighbourhood in
quarterly steps. Each is one change to the model and gets its own fit on the latest split.
The sampler draws each term's two scales with its paths integrated out (a Kalman filter over
the steps), then the paths (`rentfrontier.drift`'s docstring).

### Predictions for `drift-building` (written before its fit, and before the drift fit's scores)

1. It passes the gate. I put this at 50%: the building term and the units' drift both explain
   a unit's recent ads in a building with few units, and the two sds can trade off.
2. The building sd is 0.025 to 0.045 and the monthly rho is 0.80 to 0.93, a correlation time
   of 0.4 to 1.2 years.
3. The units' drift sd falls by 0.005 to 0.02 from the drift fit's, and sigma in the two
   smallest bedroom groups falls by a further 0.002 to 0.006.
4. Held-out elpd is 2,500 to 9,000 over the drift fit's. The gain per ad is largest for
   held-out ads whose unit last ran two or more years earlier while another unit of the
   building ran within six months.
5. The 90% intervals cover 0.89 to 0.915 of the held-out ads.
6. A sweep takes at most 0.5 s longer than the drift fit's.

### Predictions for `drift-local` (written before its fit)

1. It passes the gate at 50%, given that `drift-building` does.
2. The neighbourhood sd is 0.008 to 0.02 and the quarterly rho is 0.85 to 0.98.
3. Held-out elpd is 300 to 2,500 over `drift-building`'s. The latest split holds out a
   unit's last ad, so its held-out ads lean recent, and the neighbourhood moves are largest
   in 2025 and 2026.
4. The building term's sd and rho move by less than their posterior sds.

The model with the highest held-out elpd among those that pass the gate is fitted on the
fresh data and serves the deal scores.

## Second round: the same fits from the reviewed sampler (predictions written before launch)

The first round (F2, B1, L1, F4, F5, launched 2026-11-10 01:55Z from `00aa62e`) was still
running when the second review of PR #1281 came back with two findings that touch it:

1. The slice updates of the unit scales and of each local term's (log sd, logit rho) used
   `b0.slice_vec` with a fixed count of stepping-out moves on each side. That narrows the
   draws when the slice is longer than the stepped-out interval. The reviewer measured it on
   a normal target: the variance comes out at 0.97 of the truth when the interval is one
   posterior sd long, and 0.71 when it is a fifth of one. For the unit scales the interval is
   two sd long after the warmup adapts it, where the bias is not measurable. For the local
   terms the interval was fixed at 0.05 (log sd) and 0.2 (logit rho).
2. A row with no neighbourhood was numbered -1 and read the last neighbourhood's path
   (`drift-local` only).

`ad2f827` fixes both: the intervals only shrink, which is exact at any length, and rows with
no neighbourhood share a group. The first round is kept as a check on how much the fix
matters, and the second round, from the reviewed code, is the record.

Predictions for the second round against the first, model by model:

1. Posterior means of every scale move by less than one first-round posterior sd.
2. The posterior sd of the buildings' log sd and logit rho grows by 0% to 15%, and the
   neighbourhoods' by 5% to 40% (fewer groups, so a wider posterior and a slice longer than
   the old interval).
3. Held-out elpd changes by less than 150 in either direction for each model, and the order
   of the three models does not change.
4. The gate verdict of each model is the same in both rounds. If a model passes in one round
   and misses in the other, the miss is on a local term's rho or sd.
5. Sweep time changes by less than 10%.

## Rounds one and two: no fit passes the gate

All twelve fits ran (4 chains, 300 warmup, 1,000 draws, about 0.9 to 1.3 s a sweep, $17.56
with the pilot). None passes the gate, in either round, and the two rounds agree: the
shrink-only slice was a correctness fix and not the cause.

| Model | Round one (worst R-hat, least ESS, quantities that miss) | Round two |
|---|---|---|
| `drift`, latest | 1.071, 49, 10 | 1.032, 70, 10 |
| `drift-building`, latest | 1.650, 8, 125 | 1.647, 5, 124 |
| `drift-local`, latest | 1.492, 8, 116 | 1.536, 4, 117 |
| `drift-building`, backtest | 1.559, 12, 117 | 1.565, 9, 118 |
| `drift-building`, market | 1.631, 7, 119 | 1.601, 7, 117 |
| `drift-local`, backtest | | 1.571, 4, 114 |
| `drift-local`, market | | 1.516, 7, 111 |

Following the rule for a fit that misses the gate, nothing was rerun on another seed. No row
of any dataset lacks a neighbourhood, so the second review's finding on that changed no fit.

**`drift` misses narrowly.** The ten quantities are the four sigmas, nu, the three unit
scales and two studio coefficients. Pooled over chains: unit_sd 0.0483, drift_sd 0.0786,
drift_tau 5.56 years, nu 1.756, sigma 0.0225 to 0.0353. The sweep draws sigma given the unit
levels u, and u given sigma. With u given, 1.7 million residuals pin sigma tightly. With u
integrated out only the repeat ads of a unit inform it, so its posterior is much wider than
the step each sweep can take. The unit scales were already drawn with u integrated out, but
given sigma.

The round-two traces (1,000 sweeps a chain) fit that reading in part. Within a chain the
sigmas have the longest autocorrelation times, 20 to 84 sweeps, then nu at 23 to 46, then
the unit scales at 14 to 32. The three unit scales lie on a ridge (correlations of their
logs: unit_sd and drift_sd -0.96, unit_sd and drift_tau -0.93). The sigmas correlate only
weakly with drift_sd (+0.14 to +0.28) and more with nu (+0.59 to +0.69). So the trade-off is
between sigma and the unit levels themselves, not between sigma and the drift's scale, and
nu follows sigma.

**The building term misses badly.** building_ar_sd comes out at 0.074 and its monthly rho at
0.943 (a half-life near a year), both far from what I predicted (0.025 to 0.045, 0.80 to
0.93). With the term in the model the units' drift_sd falls from 0.079 to 0.051 and its time
scale rises from 5.6 to about 14 years: the building path takes over most of what moved the
units together. The chains agree on the means to three digits and still fail, because each
chain creeps. The path is drawn given u, u given the path, and the building level given the
path, and each of those conditionals is far tighter than the posterior.

## Round three: the scales and sigma drawn together (predictions written before launch)

`update_hyp` now draws (log ss, log sd, log tau) and the four log sigmas together, given the
mixing scales lam and with u integrated out, along directions set twice during the warmup
from the draws so far (`scale_logp` is checked against the dense density in
`test_drift.py`). The noise step still draws nu and sigma given u, with lam integrated out. The warmup is 600 sweeps,
up from 300, so that each setting of the directions has 100 sweeps behind it.

Round three is `drift` alone, on the latest split, the market dataset and the backtest.

1. The latest-split fit passes the gate. I put this at 55%. If it misses, the miss is on nu
   (drawn given u only) or on drift_tau, with R-hat under 1.03.
2. Every scale's posterior mean is within 1.5 pooled sd of round two's: unit_sd 0.0483,
   drift_sd 0.0786, drift_tau 5.56, nu 1.756.
3. The least bulk ESS among the sigmas and the unit scales rises from 70 to over 600.
4. Held-out elpd is within 100 of round two's `drift` fit.
5. A sweep takes at most 1.15 s (0.92 s in round two).
