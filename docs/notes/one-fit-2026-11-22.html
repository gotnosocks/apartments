One fit instead of three

Yes, one fit can do the work of three. Hold out the open ads plus the newest ad of 10% of relisted apartments, using only ads a year or more old. That single fit serves Deals, selection and Diagnostics, and the deal-score validity check. At screen size it costs Deals about 0.006 nats an open ad, roughly 2 SE. A model's cost drops to about a third.

The problem

Today a model needs a latest-split fit (selection, Diagnostics), a market fit (Deals) and an as-of backtest (validity of the deal score). At full length that is about $28 each.

What I tried

Each arm is one k5 screen on the backtest market dataset as of 2025-11-10, with the same model (layrng,size,sv_gp_mo,marks). The bars show the change in elpd on its 1,891 open ads against the market-only fit, with ±2 SE.

  • B: every latest row held out. Costs 53 nats.
  • C: the same, but no latest rows from apartments with an open ad. Costs 36.
  • D: C, keeping only latest rows a year or more old. Costs 12.

The reason: the latest split holds each relisted apartment's newest ad, so it takes about 27% of the last year's closed ads, against 10% overall. Holding those out starves the Deals fit of the recent market. The age filter fixes most of that.

D's median error and coverage match the market fit: RMSE 0.1138 against 0.1132, and 80% coverage 0.761 against 0.755.

Validity from the same fit

Its 26,407 year-old latest rows have known final asks, so the deal-score check runs on them. Below, by tenth of where the first ask sits in the predictive distribution, from cheap to dear:

Both rise steadily, as on the open ads of a strict backtest (Spearman 0.98 and 1.00 across tenths). There are 14 times as many ads per tenth, so the table is far tighter.

Selection still works on old rows

Re-scoring the cost arms below on only the year-old rows keeps nearly all of their power. For half-year paths, z is −10.6 on the old rows against −10.8 on all latest rows. A change that matters only in the last year would show only on the open ads, which the one fit also scores.

Cheaper sweeps: mostly no

Coarser building paths are faster but much worse; the quarterly path carries a lot. Dropping the slow step leaves the model the same but saves only 5%. None gets a full fit.

What's next
