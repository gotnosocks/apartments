You are checking NYC rental ads for a research check. Every ad in your batch was listed among
residential rentals and is used as an apartment ad. For each ad, decide what it advertises.

Read the batch file named in BATCH with the Read tool. Each ad shows its read id, its
building, the day it was posted, its coded fields (the unit label the lister typed, bedrooms,
full and half baths, any stated size) and its whole description. Below it are up to four
other ads of the same unit, and for some ads up to three ads of other units of the building,
each with its coded fields and the start of its text. Dollar figures are masked as [amount]
and no ask is given, so price is never evidence here. Use only the batch file: open no other
file and fetch nothing from the web.

Answer each ad with one of these:

- `commercial`: the advertised space itself is a store or storefront, an office, a medical or
  professional suite, a restaurant, a warehouse, a showroom, a parking space, a work studio, a
  whole building or lot for a business, or any other space not meant to be lived in.
- `placeholder`: the ad shows no real apartment listing. Its text is empty, a few
  characters, or a stub such as "Draft", "TBD" or a one-line greeting, and its coded fields
  look like a blank form's defaults: for example 0 bedrooms where the unit's other ads, or
  the building's ads of the same time, describe an apartment with 2 or 3 bedrooms.
- `apartment`: an apartment, house, townhouse floor, loft or room that people rent to live in,
  and the coded bedrooms fit what the text or the unit's other ads say (a studio coded 0 bed
  fits). An apartment in a building with stores or offices is an apartment, and so is one
  whose ad mentions shops or retail nearby, a home office, a leasing office or room to work
  from home. A short or empty text whose coded fields agree with the unit's other ads is an
  apartment. A live/work loft that people may live in is an apartment.
- `unclear`: the batch does not say enough to choose, for example an empty text with no other
  ads, or other ads that point both ways.

Judge the advertised unit, not the building or the neighborhood. A coded bedroom count does
not make a space residential on its own: listers of commercial spaces often leave the coded
fields at their defaults. A unit label such as STORE, COMMERCIAL or OFFICE is strong evidence,
and so is an ad that speaks of tenants' businesses, build-out, zoning, frontage, foot traffic
or a lease per square foot per year. Words like "storefront" or "office" that describe the
building's ground floor, the neighborhood or a room inside an apartment are not.

For each ad, quote the words that decide it (25 words at most, copied exactly from the batch;
