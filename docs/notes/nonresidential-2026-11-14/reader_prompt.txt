You are checking NYC rental ads for a research check. Every ad in your batch was listed among
residential rentals. For each ad, decide what kind of space it advertises.

Read the batch file named in BATCH with the Read tool. Each ad shows its read id, the unit
label the lister typed, its coded bedrooms and baths, its room count, any stated size, the date
its record was last collected, and its description. Dollar figures are masked as [amount] and
no ask is given, so price is never evidence here. Use only the batch file: open no other file
and fetch nothing from the web.

Answer each ad with one of these:

- `residential`: an apartment, a house, a townhouse floor or a room that people rent to live
  in. Lofts and studios count here. An apartment in a building with stores, offices or a
  commercial ground floor is residential, and so is an apartment whose ad mentions shops
  nearby, retail in the building, a home office or room to work from home.
- `commercial`: the advertised space itself is a store or storefront, an office, a medical or
  professional suite, a restaurant, a warehouse, a showroom, a parking space, a work studio or
  any other space not meant to be lived in.
- `live_work`: the advertised space is offered for both living and working, such as a
  live/work loft, and the ad allows people to live in it.
- `unclear`: the ad doesn't say enough to choose, for example an empty or generic description
  with a label and coded fields that point both ways.

Judge the advertised unit, not the building or the neighborhood. A coded bedroom count does
not make a space residential on its own: listers of commercial spaces often leave the coded
fields at their defaults. A unit label such as STORE, COMMERCIAL or OFFICE is strong evidence,
and so is an ad that speaks of tenants' businesses, build-out, zoning, frontage or a lease per
square foot per year. Words like "storefront" or "office space" describing the building's
ground floor or the neighborhood are not.

For each ad, quote the words that decide it (25 words at most, copied from the batch) and give
a one-sentence reason.

Write one JSON object per line, one line per ad, in read id order, to the file named in
LABELS, using the Write tool:

{"read_id": "R01", "answer": "commercial", "quote": "...", "why": "..."}

The answer must be one of residential, commercial, live_work or unclear. Then reply with the
number of lines you wrote and nothing else.
