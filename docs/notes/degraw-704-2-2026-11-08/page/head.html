A 2-bed, 1-bath apartment on the second floor of a three-family 1901 brownstone in Park Slope, listed at $4,700 on 11 June 2026 and off the market five days later. The served model never saw that ad: the run's random row split held it out. It did learn from three earlier ads for the same unit, from 2019, 2020 and 2023. Every number below comes from the model's saved draws (1,000 draws over 2 chains). The model puts the ask at $5,231, 80% range $4,574 to $5,968, so $4,700 falls at the 15th percentile. Nearby 2-bed walk-ups asked a median $4,500 that summer. No term shows its two chains disagreeing.

What could move this

  • The building's own level, walk and slopes together add +0.20 log points (×1.22), 95% from +0.02 to +0.40. The model learned them from this unit's three training ads, the only rows it holds for the building. The 2019 ad asked above the model (97th percentile) and the 2023 ad asked a little above it. These terms are most of the gap between the model's $5,231 and the $4,500 median of nearby walk-ups.
  • From January 2024 to June 2026 the ask went from $4,500 to $4,700 (+4%). Over the same months the model's market trend rose by 0.13 log points (+14%), and the model passes that rise to the unit in full. In January 2024 the model predicted $4,370 for the held-out $4,500 ad, which was close.
  • The ad came off the market five days after it went live. A quick rental fits an ask set at or under what the apartment could fetch. It could also mean the landlord priced for a fast fill. The ad offers no concession.
  • No ad states a size, so the size-unknown column takes 2.4% off. PLUTO lists 2,700 residential sq ft over three apartments, about 900 sq ft each. 679 Degraw #2, across the street at 950 sq ft, asked $4,900 the next day. I changed no field.
  • Every ad says "2nd floor", but the coded floor is unresolved, so the floor-unknown column applies (+0.7%). Coding floor 2 would move the price by about a percent. Ad text alone is no grounds for a correction under the project's rules.
  • The 2020 ad fell from $3,850 to $2,790 in six cuts during the pandemic. The model counts only the last ask, below the 2nd percentile of its prediction. That row pulls the unit's own effect down and the building terms have to make up the rest.
