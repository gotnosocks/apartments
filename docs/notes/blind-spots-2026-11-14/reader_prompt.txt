You are reading NYC rental ads for a research check. Read the file BATCH with the Read tool. It
holds 20 JSON lines, one ad each, with these keys:

- `read_id`;
- `address`: the building's key, a street address and borough;
- `unit_label`: the unit's name in the ad's address (4B, PHA, GARDEN), upper-cased, or empty;
- `neighborhood` and `date`, the day the ad's rent was set;
- `coded_fields`: everything a pricing model knows about this apartment besides its location
  and its earlier ads. A field missing from `coded_fields` is unknown to the model, and a
  missing flag (`flag_furnished`, `label_penthouse` and so on) is false. `size_used_sqft` is the
  size the model uses (`size_source` says where it came from), `stated_sqft` the size the ad
  states. The code lists (`unit_features`, `private_outdoor`, `views`, `building_amenities` and
  the rest) are the broker's coded checkboxes;
- `description`: the ad's latest text, with every dollar figure replaced by [amount].

For each ad, name the single most likely quality of this apartment or this ad that a renter
would price, paying more or less for it, and that the coded fields miss or get wrong. Pick a
name from this list, or write your own short snake_case name when none fits:

- duplex_multilevel: the unit spans two or more levels
- penthouse_top_floor: a penthouse or top-floor unit the fields don't mark as one
- private_outdoor: private terrace, balcony, yard or roof the fields miss or understate
- views: notable views the fields miss
- renovation: renovation state, such as a gut renovation or a unit needing work
- furnished_short_term: furnished, short-term, corporate or hotel-like rental
- shared_room: a room or bed in a shared apartment, or shared kitchen or bath
- income_restricted_program: income-restricted, lottery, AMI, HPD, Mitchell-Lama, voucher or
  another program
- garden_basement: garden-level, basement or below-grade unit
- size_mismatch: a unit far larger or smaller than its bedroom count suggests
- layout_fault: windowless or interior bedroom, flex or pressurized walls, railroad layout
- luxury_building_missed: a luxury, doorman or full-service building the fields miss
- utilities_fees_included: utilities, internet or other fees included in the rent, or
  extra fees on top of it
- data_error: beds, baths, unit or price wrong in the coded fields, for instance a text with a
  different bedroom count, several units in one ad, a whole house, a sale, a commercial
  space, a parking spot, or a price that isn't a month's rent for the whole apartment
- none_apparent: nothing a renter would price is missing from the fields

Rules:

1. Pick only a quality the coded fields miss or get wrong. If a code already says it (the
   `private_outdoor` list has TERRACE and the text mentions a terrace), it doesn't count,
   unless the text shows it is far beyond what the code implies, such as a 1,000 square foot
   terrace. Say so in `cause_note` then.
2. For `data_error`, the evidence shows the conflict, such as "3 bedroom" in the text against
   a bedroom count of 1.
3. When the description is empty, or says nothing beyond the fields, answer `none_apparent`
   unless the fields alone show a data error.
4. Judge the advertised unit. If the text lists several units, say `data_error` when it is
   unclear which unit the fields describe.
5. Ignore marketing words that every ad uses ("stunning", "sun-filled", "great location").

Write one JSON line per ad to OUT with these keys:

- `read_id`;
- `cause`: a list name or your own snake_case name;
- `cause_note`: at most 15 words saying exactly what you found, required when you write your
  own name;
- `evidence`: a quote of at most 25 words from the description, or the conflicting fields, or
  "" for `none_apparent`;
- `direction`: "raises" or "lowers", for whether this quality puts this apartment's rent above
  or below what the coded fields alone suggest, or "unclear" for `none_apparent` and when you
  can't tell;
- `confidence`: "high", "medium" or "low".

Write OUT with the Write tool as plain JSON lines, exactly one per input line, in input order.
Open no file other than BATCH. Then reply only with the number of lines written and the count
of each cause. Don't reply with the labels themselves.
