# Bedroom-text flag: a unit column that stays fixed across a unit's ads

The bedroom-text pool (`bed-text-2026-11-16.md`) leads L2 by 223.6 held-out elpd and loses
52.3 on the 184,013 ads without the flag. This note traces that loss to the unit column and
sets up a variant of it. No fit is launched from this note.

## The loss sits in units with flagged earlier ads

`bed-text-2026-11-16/split.py` rebuilds the unit column of the bedtext model, the mean of
the flag over the unit's earlier training ads, and splits the held-out ads by it:

| Held-out ads | Ads | elpd, bedtext minus L2 | SE | mean miss, L2 | mean miss, bedtext |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no flag, unit mean 0 | 178,725 | +54.6 | 23.2 | | |
| no flag, unit mean above 0 | 5,288 | -106.9 | 14.6 | -0.0009 | -0.0058 |
| flag, unit mean below 1 | 3,054 | +171.3 | 22.8 | -0.0292 | -0.0083 |
| flag, unit mean 1 | 1,968 | +106.5 | 13.8 | -0.0184 | -0.0018 |

A miss is the log ask minus the median. The table leaves out the 612 held-out ads whose
text states more bedrooms than coded, so its rows sum to 225.5 against 223.6 over all ads.

L2 priced the 5,288 unflagged ads of flagged units almost without bias. The bedtext model
raises their medians by 0.005 on average and scores 107 worse on them. Its unit coefficient
of -0.059 times their average unit mean of 0.355 would lower them by 0.021, so the rest of
the model, mostly the unit levels, moved them up by about 0.026. My reading: the model
lowers a unit's flagged rows through both coefficients, the unit's level rises in answer,
and an unflagged ad receives the raised level with only a share of the unit coefficient.
The unit mean also changes from one ad of a unit to the next as flagged ads accumulate.

## The variant

`drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedunit` keeps `ad:bed_text_smaller` and replaces the
unit mean with `uever:ad:bed_text_smaller`. I set it to 1 on a row carrying the flag and on
every row of a unit with a flagged training ad, and to 0 elsewhere. It is the only change
from the bedtext model, on the same rows, dataset copy and split. The column reads flags of
training rows and the row's own flag, and no rent.

Row counts on the latest split: 51,628 of 1,706,824 training rows carry the flag and 86,241
have the unit column at 1. Among held-out ads, 5,022 carry the flag and 5,316 more have the
unit column at 1 without it.

The column is constant over a unit's training ads, so its coefficient moves the centre of
the unit levels for those units and the ad column carries the within-unit difference.

## The fit, when approved

Three H100 containers as for the bedtext pool, scored against that pool with
`bed-text-2026-11-16/cmp.py`:

```sh
D=/data1/apartments/frontier/datasets/b0-b3ddebf19af1dc1e-alldata-v36-dense-nyc-closingraise-bedtext
IN=/data1/apartments/pipeline/datasets/rows-b3ddebf19af1dc1e.parquet
ops/modal-fit --outside-budget --gpu H100 --dataset "$D" --input "$IN" --split latest \
  --baseline drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedtext-b0-latest-c1ffaefd-l2bt COMMIT l2bu-a \
  drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedunit b0 2 500 9000 40 --seed 1 --level-dof 4 \
  --no-loo --no-heldout
```

with seeds 2 and 3 as `l2bu-b` and `l2bu-c`. I then pool the three and score the held-out
ads with `python -m rentfrontier.drift_score`, as for the bedtext pool. The pool costs
about $11. I hold it until Ben
answers on the short-fit test of `short-chains-2026-11-16.md`, since a short fit would
answer the same question for about $1.25.

## Predictions (written before any fit)

1. The pool passes the gate (80%).
2. `ad:bed_text_smaller` has a posterior mean between -0.035 and -0.015 (65%), further
   from 0 than the bedtext model's -0.005 (85%).
3. `uever:ad:bed_text_smaller` has a posterior mean between -0.03 and 0.005 (60%).
4. On the 5,316 unflagged held-out ads with the unit column at 1 (the last group
   `split.py` prints), the variant recovers at least 60 of the 106.0 elpd the bedtext
   model lost to L2 (50%). The ad coefficient still lowers a unit's flagged rows, so the
   unit's level can still rise in answer.
5. On the 5,022 flagged held-out ads, the variant keeps at least 200 of the bedtext
   model's 278 over L2 (60%). The past mean separated a unit's first flagged ad from its
   later ones, and the fixed column gives that up.
6. Over all held-out ads the variant leads the bedtext pool (55%), and the difference lies
   between -60 and +120 (75%).

## Reading the result

A lead over the bedtext pool of more than 2 paired SE makes the variant the best model on
the latest split. A tie or a loss keeps the bedtext model and closes this question, with the
107 recorded as a cost of the unit mean.

## The fits, as short fits

Ben approved the variant's fit on Nov 16, as a short fit if the test of
`short-chains-2026-11-16.md` held. It held: a single container with 500 warmup sweeps and
320 kept sweeps of 2 chains scored 89 elpd below L2's pool for $1.37. Two short fits
can differ by about 60 elpd from sampling noise, and a short fit sits below its pool by an
amount I have measured once. So I compare short fits with short fits and run the bedtext
model as a short fit too. The commands are the one above with `2 500 320 1` in place of
`2 500 9000 40`, the baseline `drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-b0-latest-75fe7f29-l2short`,
and

- `bt-short` for `drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedtext`,
- `bu-short` for `drift-building-phase-unitkn-level-bedunit`,

each with seed 1. I score both with `drift_score` and compare them with each other and with
L2's short fit `l2short` using `bed-text-2026-11-16/cmp.py` and `split.py`.

Predictions 2 to 6 above stand, scored on the short fits, with L2's and the bedtext
model's short fits in place of their pools. Prediction 1 is not scored, since a short fit
misses the scalar gate by construction. Added before the launch:

7. The bedtext short fit leads L2's short fit by 150 to 300 held-out elpd (70%), against
   the pools' 223.6. This checks that two short fits reproduce a lead the pools measured.
8. The two containers settle between $2.00 and $3.20 together (75%).
9. The bedtext short fit scores 40 to 140 below the bedtext pool's 237,654.3 (65%), as
   L2's short fit scored 89 below L2's pool. This checks that the offset of a short fit
   from its pool is about equal across models.

For these short fits the rule of the section above gives way to a wider one: a difference
between the variant and the bedtext model under 100 elpd reads as a tie, whatever its
paired SE. The sharper test is the last group `split.py` prints, the 5,316 unflagged
held-out ads whose unit has a flagged training ad of any day, where the bedtext pool lost
106.0 to L2 with an SE of 14.7. That group differs from the table's 5,288 ads, which
count only flagged ads earlier than the held-out ad and leave out ads stating more
bedrooms.

## Reading

The bedtext model with its past-mean unit column stays the candidate, and the `bedunit`
variant is dropped. A unit's flagged and unflagged ads do not share a single lower level:
a column constant over the unit moves the flagged ads of mixed units too far and still
costs 68 elpd on the unit's unflagged ads. The past mean scales the shift by the share of
the unit's earlier ads carrying the flag, and that fits the flagged ads better by 130 elpd.

The 102 elpd the bedtext model loses on unflagged ads in flagged units remains. A column
that would address it has to tell a unit's flagged ads from its unflagged ones in the
unit's level, which the past mean does only in part.

Predictions 7 and 9 came out right, so two short fits reproduced a lead the pools
measured to within 13 elpd, and the offset of a short fit below its pool was 76 and 89 for
the two models with pools.
