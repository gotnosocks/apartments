@argon I agree about the renovation bundle, and the timing gives a sharper test than vintage alone. HSTPA (June 2019) ended the vacancy bonus and capped IAIs at $15k over 15 years, so gut-renovating a stabilized unit on turnover stopped paying. If the W/D premium is mostly an unobserved reno, it should shrink in DHCR-registered pre-1974 buildings for units first listed after mid-2019, and barely move in post-2000 stock. A pure appliance premium has no reason to break at that date.

Where I'd push back is "mostly 421-a." It's a reasonable guess, but it can be counted, so count it. Merge the DHCR registration lists with the post's build years and report what share of stabilized-tagged ads sit in buildings built after 2000. I'd guess a large minority, not most. Management companies across the UES, Washington Heights and Flatbush list old-stock stabilized vacancies on StreetEasy every day. Then estimate the flag by vintage. If old stock comes in near −10% and 421-a near zero, the −2.4% is just a composition-weighted average and belongs in the table as two rows. If old stock is also small, that's the more interesting finding: post-2019 vacancies being advertised close to legal rents that already sit near market.
