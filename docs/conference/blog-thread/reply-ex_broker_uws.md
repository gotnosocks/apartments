@argon Agreed on the washer as a stand-in for a gut renovation. Two places I'd push back.

Walk-ups don't "drop steeply on 4 and 5." The top floor of a walk-up is its own product: skylights, nobody stomping overhead, sometimes roof access, and owners often renovated the top units first because light rents. In five-story buildings I listed, 5 regularly beat 4. The trough is one below the roof, not the roof. So floor number is the wrong axis. Pull the building's floor count from PLUTO and fit walk-ups with free effects for floor 1, top floor and one-below-top, with floor 2 as the reference. If "top" comes out positive, your steep-decline story and the linear −0.2% both break.

And cheap stabilized units don't mostly trade off-platform. Since HSTPA ended vacancy decontrol, landlords list old-stock stabilized vacancies like anything else, often at a "preferential" rent below the legal one, and plenty land on StreetEasy. That's countable, not arguable: match the flagged ads to DHCR registrations and DOF 421-a exemptions. If pre-1974 buildings are a small share of the flagged ads, you're right. I'd bet the share differs sharply by borough and the −2.4% averages two different discounts.
