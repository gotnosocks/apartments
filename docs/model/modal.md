# Modal posterior fitting

## Frontier fits on Modal

Since 2026-10-06 (Ben), frontier fits may run on a Modal GPU, **within a dollar budget that accrues at the cost of 10 full fits a day** (Ben, 2026-10-07). `ops/modal-fit` takes drive.sh's arguments, runs the fit and its PSIS-LOO in one Modal container, and puts the results where a thelio fit's go:

```sh
ops/modal-fit [--gpu A100-40GB] [--dataset DIR] [--input PATH]... [--split rows] [--chain-batch N] \
  COMMIT LABEL MODEL FEATURES CHAINS WARMUP DRAWS KEEP [rentfrontier.run options...]
python3 ops/modal/cap.py                       # the Modal balance, recent launches, when a full fit may start
ops/team/wait-next --unit 'modal-fit-*'        # wake when it lands
```

If our estimate is too low, the accumulation should continue from the negative balance." The balance started at $0 at 21:59Z that day and accrues 10 × the estimated cost of the served full fit (2 × (300 + 4,500) on an A100-40GB, about $0.88, so $8.80 a day; Ben 22:32Z: "Raise the Modal accrual and cap to $8.80 a day (10 × $0.88)."; from 2026-10-10 16:25Z, $10 a day, Ben: "OK: run the fit queue at $10/day"). Ben (22:23Z): "I would like to cap the balance on the dollar budget at ~10 full fits", so accrual stops while the balance is at one day's accrual ($10 since 2026-10-10), and a refund at settlement can't lift it over. A fit's estimate is the GPU container's list price × (800 s + the fit stage), plus 240 s of the new-building container at its list price ($2.53 an hour). The fit stage runs at 0.42 s per iteration per pair of chains, or at the model's own rate on its last Modal fit of the same model and feature set if that was slower (`measured_rate`, from the run's `modal/modal.json` and `result.json`). The A100 fits of 2026-10-12..13 calibrate it: they spent 580 to 784 s outside the fit stage, 0.30 to 0.50 s an iteration in it, and 202 to 227 s in the new-building container. A served full fit comes to $2.03 and 3,056 s (51 min) at the default rate, and C at its own 0.48 s to $2.23 and 3,347 s. Fits whose summary bundle hit its 1,200 s cap ran 3,020 to 4,154 s in all and settled above their estimate. The calibration was 700 s + 0.42 s with no new-building container ($1.80) until 2026-10-14, and 800 s + 0.11 s ($0.88) on the NB5 data. The $8.80 rate and old launcher rows stay priced at $0.88 (`NB5_SERVED_FIT_USD`), so the history isn't repriced. Before its container starts, a launch is charged its estimate in `/data1/apartments/modal/ledger.jsonl` and refused if the balance is short (`ops/modal/cap.py`). When it returns, it is settled at its container time × list price (or the time it ran, if it failed), so an overrun leaves the balance lower, possibly negative, and accrual continues from there. A queue script polls `python3 ops/modal/cap.py --check CHAINS WARMUP DRAWS` before calling `ops/modal-fit`. The per-fit cap below is separate from this balance.
- **Per-fit cap.** Ben (2026-10-14 09:57Z): "I'm pretty hard capped at about 1 hour or maybe $2 of modal compute." `cap.py` refuses a launch whose estimated Modal time is over `PER_FIT_SECONDS` (3,600 s), however much balance there is: `reserve()` raises before charging anything, `--check-launch` (ops/modal-fit's pre-check) and `--check` exit 2 with the reason, so a queue script can tell a fit it must redesign from one that only waits for dollars (exit 1). `--check` estimates at the default rate; `--check-launch` and `fit.py` read the model's own. The cap applies to the estimate before launch, not to the cost a fit settles at. Ben confirmed on 2026-10-14 (18:42Z) that the cap is the hour and that C-sized fits at about $2.60 are fine, so there is no per-fit dollar cap: `PER_FIT_USD` is None and no launch is refused on its dollar estimate. C-sized fits settled at $1.61 to $2.75 on 2026-10-10..13. The daily accrual under **Budget** is unchanged. C passes at 3,347 s. At the same size, areatime (0.70 s an iteration on 2026-10-11) comes to 4,380 s and is refused, as is the 24,000-draw AR(1) walk plan (11,246 s at the default rate).
- **Large fits.** Ben (2026-10-16 06:37Z) asked for a fit on all the scraped data (`data.DATASET_ALL`, 1.75M rows, about 4.9 times NB9) with no 1-hour limit. `ops/modal-fit --large` runs it in a container of its own: an A100-80GB or H100 with 8 CPUs and 192 GiB, the fit capped at 9 h and the container at 12 h. `cap.py` prices it at that container's rate (`USD_PER_HOUR_LARGE`) and leaves it out of `PER_FIT_SECONDS`. Until a large fit of the same model and feature set has a measured rate, `cap.py` prices it at 2.1 s an iteration per pair of chains, the default 0.42 s scaled by the 4.9 times NB9's fit rows. A short probe fit then fits the balance, while the full 2 × 4,800-iteration fit comes to about $17 on an H100, which is over the $10 ceiling and can't launch from the daily budget. A large fit skips the new-building container, whose 128 GiB and hour don't stretch to it. The container saves the run and its LOO to the Volume right after LOO, before the post-fit statistics, and keeps 45 minutes before its timeout for the final copy. The summary bundle and its calibration run in the same container after the fit and LOO, since thelio's 15 GB can't hold a bundle that size, and `fit.py` starts no `modal-summary` unit for it.

- **Publishing a large run later.** When a large fit stops before its summary, or its summary needs a gate override, `ops/modal-fit --large --publish-only --run NAME` builds the summary bundle, calibration, rent map and kit in a large container from the run and LOO still on the Volume under `/out/NAME`. `--allow-failing` passes the gate override to the summary and kit, and those record it. The launcher stages the dataset named in the run's `result.json`, and refuses before reserving anything if `/out/NAME/run` is gone from the Volume. The container runs the summary, kit, calibration and rent map in that order. When the summary fails, it skips the kit and calibration, since both read the bundle, and it starts no step once 4 hours have passed. The publish exits 0 only if every step succeeded. The container copies each finished output to `/out/NAME-publish`, and `fit.py` downloads it to the usual `summaries/`, `calibration/`, `maps/` and `kits/` folders on thelio, with the log and `publish.json` under `runs/NAME/modal/`. `cap.py` reserves 3 hours of the large container for it.
- **Saving the design.** On the all-scraped data the prep (`rentfrontier.design`) took 15 minutes in the served run's large fit container, and every publish step repeats it. On thelio a summary of the served all-scraped run hit a 9 GB cap during the prep and was killed (2026-10-17). With `ops/modal-fit --save-design`, the container copies the run's design pickle to `/out/NAME/design/` right after the fit, leaving out any design that came in with the staged inputs. `fit.py` downloads it to `OUTPUT_ROOT/designs/` for a fit that passed, outside `cache/`, which every fit uploads as an input. It skips the download for a large fit, which gets no summary on thelio. The `modal-summary` unit then runs with `FRONTIER_DESIGN_CACHE=read`: the summary loads the pickle from `designs/` when its key matches, and otherwise, or when the pickle won't load, builds the design without writing one. A `--publish-only` container copies `/out/NAME/design/` into its own cache before the summary. The key covers the package's sources and config files, so it matches only when the summary's checkout holds the same ones as the fit's. It leaves out `FRONTIER_COMMIT`, `FRONTIER_DESIGN_CACHE`, `FRONTIER_RANGE_*` and `FRONTIER_OUTPUT_ROOT`, and hashes the resolved output root instead, so the fit's container and the thelio unit come to the same key. The pickles stay in `designs/` until someone removes them.
- **Preparing a design alone.** For a run already downloaded whose prep runs out of thelio's memory, `ops/modal-fit --design-only --run NAME COMMIT x x x 0 0 0 0` prepares its design in a CPU container (4 cores, 64 GiB, an hour's timeout) at COMMIT. Use the commit the summary will check out, since the key covers the package and config files. The launcher stages the dataset named in the run's `result.json` and sends the record along, and the container runs `python -m rentfrontier.design NAME` with `FRONTIER_DESIGN_CACHE=1`, as the summary loads the data. It copies the new pickle to `/out/NAME-design/design/`, and `fit.py` downloads it to `OUTPUT_ROOT/designs/`, with the log and `design.json` (exit, pickle bytes, the container's memory peak) under `runs/NAME/modal/`. A summary at the same commit run with `FRONTIER_DESIGN_CACHE=read` and no other keyed `FRONTIER_*` variable, such as `FRONTIER_EXTERNAL_ROOT`, then loads it. The CLI prints the full key, `design.json` keeps that record, and a container whose prep saved no new pickle exits nonzero. `cap.py` reserves the container's full hour, about $0.70, and settles at the time it ran.
- **Which fit a test gets.** Ben (2026-10-07 22:24Z): "Cap the Modal balance at one day's accrual. Run every rule or feature test on Modal as its own fit, run a full fit and serve if it passes the gate. No thelio pairs, no bundling." Then (22:27Z): "Scratch that run all as full fits, serve on the app if they pass the gate." The cap is the ceiling under **Budget**. Every data-rule, feature or model-term test gets a Modal full fit of its own on the served design (2 × (300 + 4,500) since the 3,600-draw serving refit missed ESS 400 by 0.1), with no exploration step first. Each one that lands goes through autoselect, which serves the best fit that passes the gate on master's current rules. A rule fit can only be served once its rule PR merges.
- **The gate.** `run.py` checks convergence after every fit and records the result under `diagnostics` in `result.json`. A fit passes when
  1. split R-hat is at most 1.01 and bulk ESS is over 400 for the scalars, the coefficients, every 12th trend point, every building effect and 32 randomly traced unit effects;
  2. R-hat from the running sums is under 1.05 for every element of every effect.

  Ben chose to check every building on 2026-10-14 at 21:10Z. Until then the first check covered 32 randomly traced buildings, and the wing-rule C failed or passed on whichever buildings the random draw picked. To check them all, `collect.py` traces every building's effect at every draw in float32 (195 MB of device memory on C's 5,414 buildings and 2 × 4,500 draws), and `run.py` computes each building's split R-hat and bulk ESS in about 7 s. `posterior.npz` keeps those statistics as `rhat/building` and `ess/building` (43 KB), and `trace/building` still holds the 32 traced buildings. `diagnostics.buildings` counts the buildings checked, those over 1.01 and those at or under 400. Fits from before the change keep the result of the old check.
- **When a fit fails the gate.** We never rerun a failed fit on a new seed (Ben, 2026-10-14 20:45Z). A convergence failure points to a problem in what we fit, so we look for it in this order:
  1. the data, for example the rows, units, labels and price histories behind the effects that failed to mix. Ben's rule from 09:04Z that day applies: fix a data issue first, and patch the model only after that.
  2. the model structure, for example a term that trades off against another term.
  3. the sampler settings, on the assumption that the current ones are reasonable.

  On 2026-10-14 the wing-rule C failed the gate on one traced building effect. Its new-seed rerun was stopped at Ben's request 14 minutes in, at a cost of $0.58.
- **What runs.** `ops/modal-fit` starts `ops/modal/fit.py` in a `systemd-run --user` unit `modal-fit-<time>`, logging to `/data1/apartments/modal/logs/`. It ships a shallow git checkout of COMMIT, uploads only the inputs that changed since the last launch (the dataset, `/data1/apartments/external`, the frontier cache and descriptions, and a few files under `data/model/`), and runs `rentfrontier.run` then `rentfrontier.loo` in the container. The fit alone is capped at 1 h (30 min for an exploration fit, chosen as in drive.sh), and the container at 75 min as a backstop for a runaway, past the 69 min of the slowest C-sized fit so far.
- **Results.** The run directory lands in `FRONTIER_OUTPUT_ROOT/runs/<name>`, with the container log and `modal.json` (GPU, stage times, list-price estimate) under `modal/`, and the LOO directory in `FRONTIER_OUTPUT_ROOT/loo/`. Both are then deleted from the Volume. The variance decomposition of a full fit is light work; run it on thelio from a checkout of COMMIT as drive.sh does (`JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu uv run python -m rentfrontier.variance NAME`).
- **New-building score.** After a fit finishes, `fit.py` starts a second container: `app.newbuilding_cpu`, with 32 cores and 128 GiB. It copies the run from the Volume, runs `rentfrontier.newbuilding --workers 32`, and leaves the result for the download into `FRONTIER_OUTPUT_ROOT/newbuilding/`. Its cost goes into the fit's `usd_estimate`. If the container fails, `fit.py` records the error under `newbuilding` in `modal.json` and keeps the fit. To score a run already on thelio, use `ops/modal/newbuilding.py [--only FILE | --holdout] RUN [COMMIT]` with the Modal venv. It uploads the run directory, charges the balance a $0.45 estimate and settles at the container's cost. On the final-ask fit of 2026-10-13 (121,035 rows), the container spent 81 s staging and 119 s scoring, and cost $0.14. Thelio's single process took about 1,120 s on C's 193,376 rows.
- **Summary bundle.** Ben (2026-10-14 15:26Z) moved the summary and calibration out of the container: in it, the bundle hit its 1,200 s cap on several fits and added that time to the bill. After a full-tier fit of the row split (or `--split all`) is down, `fit.py` starts `ops/modal/summarize RUN` in a `systemd-run --user` unit of its own, `modal-summary-<time>-<pid>`, logging to `/data1/apartments/modal/logs/<unit>.log`. The fit's unit ends without waiting for it, so a queue runner can launch the next fit, and none of its time is charged to the Modal balance. The script runs `rentfrontier.summary` under `ops/job gpu -m 5G` from `MODAL_FIT_REPO` (serve/master for the queue runners), which refuses a run that fails the gate, then `rentfrontier.calibration` on the new bundle as a light job. If the unit fails to start, `fit.py` prints the error and keeps the fit; run the script by hand with the same `FRONTIER_OUTPUT_ROOT` and `FRONTIER_DATASET`. Exploration fits and the unit and latest splits get no bundle, since autoselect serves none of them.
- **Fit time.** A Modal fit's time is not comparable with a thelio fit's: the served design took 822 s on an A100-40GB against 6,578 s on the RTX 2060 SUPER. `result.json` records the GPU under `hardware`.
- **Cleanup.** Inputs and code stay on Volume `apartments-modal-fits` between fits. `apartments-modal-cleanup.timer` checks hourly and deletes the Volume once 24 h have passed since the last launch returned and no fit app is running; the next launch uploads everything again (about 450 MB).

### Choosing a GPU

The sampler runs in float64, so the GPU's float64 throughput sets the speed. A100-40GB is the default. On 2026-10-06 the served design (94,453 rows, 2 chains, 300 warmup and 3,600 draws) ran:

| GPU | fit | PSIS-LOO | billed |
|---|---|---|---|
| A100-40GB | 822 to 919 s (warmup 288 to 364, sampling 516 to 546) | 139 s, 106,764.6 ± 334 | $0.72 for the fit alone; $0.80 for a 1,188 s rerun against a $0.79 estimate |
| L4 (300 warmup, 180 draws) | warmup 518 s | | |
| RTX 2060 SUPER (thelio) | 6,578 s | 141 s | |

The A100 reproduced thelio's diagnostics and PSIS-LOO exactly (R-hat 1.00529, minimum ESS 469.07, held-out ELPD 13,139.0, PSIS-LOO 106,764.6 ± 334). PSIS-LOO gains nothing from the A100 (139 s against 141 s) but costs only about $0.09 inside the fit's container, so it stays there. Prices are in `ops/modal/app.py`, from [modal.com/pricing](https://modal.com/pricing).

## Root-project fits

A full-length remote refit of the product-scope spline model ran in 55.4 minutes against about 56 locally, reproduced the local protocol hash, and was billed $0.38 ([September 23 record](../analysis/modal-remote-fitting-2026-09-23.md)). One Modal core samples at local speed and the disk sampler uses at most four, so Modal helps only to run several fits at once or to keep load off the local machine.

```sh
# Main model through the CLI: same runner, same options, same protocol hash as a local run.
uv run --locked --extra model --extra modal apartments fit-pricing DATASET OUTPUT --executor modal [--modal-detach]
# Other protocol runners (e.g. structure or bedroom-time experiments):
uv run --locked --extra model --extra modal python -m models.modal_remote_fit run --detach \
  --runner models.<runner> --dataset DATASET --output OUTPUT -- <runner options>
```

`--executor modal` passes every `fit-pricing` option explicitly to the runner, because `fit-pricing` defaults differ from the runners' own; it supports only `--execution disk`. `--modal-cpu`, `--modal-memory`, `--modal-timeout`, `--modal-full` and `--modal-detach` are refused for local runs. Downloads leave `posterior.nc`/`prior.nc` on the Modal Volume unless `--modal-full` is given; run `python -m models.modal_remote_fit complete --output OUTPUT` before promoting a fit or opening it on the main page. Local runs never import Modal or need `--extra modal`. Code is sent with `git ls-files`, so run from a git checkout (jj-only workspaces are not supported yet).

## Run from the prepared cloud snapshot

Install the Modal client using the project's `modal` extra, then authenticate once with `uv run --locked --extra modal modal setup`. The [remote archive processing workflow](../data/modal-processing.md) writes `training_data.parquet` and `metadata.json` to Volume `chelsea-archive`, under `/snapshots/{snapshot}/prepared/`.

```sh
# Cloud CPU is the cost-conscious default.
uv run --locked --extra modal modal run models/modal_fit.py --snapshot chelsea-20260908 --draws 1000 --tune 1000 --chains 4

# Optional CUDA/Nutpie experiment; otherwise identical model.
uv run --locked --extra modal modal run models/modal_fit.py --snapshot chelsea-20260908 --gpu --draws 1000 --tune 1000 --chains 4

# Small remote execution test, optionally streaming results for local browsing.
uv run --locked --extra modal modal run models/modal_fit.py --snapshot chelsea-20260908 --draws 20 --tune 20 --chains 2 --output data/model/modal/cloud-smoke
```

Results persist in Volume `/fits/{run_id}/`; each run gets a unique ID unless `--run-id` is supplied. Existing IDs and local output directories are rejected. The worker reads prepared inputs directly from the Volume, validates their digest and retains snapshot provenance. There is no cloud-to-laptop-to-cloud data processing step. The laptop does not import pandas, parse parquet, build models, or load posterior arrays. Optional downloads use bounded chunks.

The fit image includes only the two model Python files and pinned scientific dependencies. It does not upload the project directory, archive, credentials or logs. The mounted Volume already contains the archive; the fitting function reads only its selected prepared inputs and writes its own fit directory. It does not import or modify the archive database.

Returned NetCDF, parquet tables and metadata use the analysis app's existing format; the production monthly fit is never replaced automatically. `input.parquet`, input/model hashes, source preparation metadata, sampler settings, dependency versions, timing and diagnostics accompany the result. `environment.txt` records resolved packages. Source unit labels are not necessarily resolved physical unit identities; inspect the preparation quality flags before interpreting a fit.

## Limits and backend details

Both workers cap CPU at four cores and host memory at 8 GiB. Each function allows one container at a time, has a 30-minute timeout, and does not automatically retry failures. `modal run` ends its ephemeral app afterward; there is no idle endpoint. Interrupting the attached command stops the app. GPU execution uses an attached nested app: do not rely on outer `modal run --detach` to keep that GPU job alive after the local command disconnects. Keep the GPU command connected until it returns. For a larger fit, review and explicitly increase the timeout instead of silently retrying.

As checked September 8, 2026, [Nutpie supports JAX GPU compilation](https://pymc-devs.github.io/nutpie/pymc-usage.html). PyMC 6.2 accepts `pm.sample(nuts_sampler='nutpie', backend='jax')`; the older nested `nuts_sampler_kwargs` syntax is deprecated. The CPU image pins PyMC 6.2.0, Nutpie 0.16.11 and PyTensor 3.2.3. The optional GPU image adds JAX 0.11.1 with [the recommended pip CUDA 13 wheels](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/installation.html). This combination successfully ran on a Modal T4. GPU runs fail if CUDA or float64 is unavailable. PyTensor constructs gradients, and JAX compiles and executes those gradients on the GPU. The local `fit_model` API also exposes `gradient_backend` for future controlled experiments; an interrupted JAX-autodiff comparison did not establish a benefit, so the tested default remains PyTensor gradients.

Both backends use float64. Cheap GPUs have limited float64 throughput and transfer/dispatch overhead, so GPU speedups are not assumed. Compare effective samples per second and per dollar, convergence and total elapsed time on identical workloads. Short smoke diagnostics are not reliable convergence estimates.

[Modal rates](https://modal.com/pricing) were $0.000164/T4-second, $0.0000131/CPU-core-second and $0.00000222/GiB-second. At the requested limits, 30 minutes of worker compute is approximately $0.13 CPU-only or $0.43 with T4, excluding startup, builds, storage and transfer. The completed T4 smoke estimated $0.027 worker compute. A job's metadata records its own runtime and estimate; these are estimates, not billing records.
