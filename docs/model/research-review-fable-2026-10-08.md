## Headline

1. **Modeling's main finding is right, and the numbers make it sharper than they said.** PSIS-LOO integrates the unit effect out but leaves the building effect in (`loo.py`), so a feature that is constant within a building can only score on rows whose building has almost no other rows. A thought experiment with the repo's scales (section 2) says a building-level feature that explains a fifth of all building variance would show about +34 ELPD, inside the ±15 SE of every location test so far, while the same feature would shrink the building scale by about 8 posterior SDs. The location nulls are a property of the score, not evidence that location features are worthless. Modeling's "explained share" is the right fix, and it needs a null reference (section 3).
2. **The free screen can give the new-building answer too.** Regressing the served fit's building levels on a candidate with buildings held out (grouped cross-validation inside the screen) is the same question as Modeling's 5-fold new-building ELPD, at no Modal cost. Reserve the 5 fits for the one or two survivors.
3. **The largest untested lever is the target itself.** 42% of ads cut their price while listed (median −3.5%) and the model only ever sees the initial ask. That is why prevprice "worked" and why the residual tails are heavy. A fit on the final ask, or with the final ask as a second flagged observation, is one Modal fit and decides whether the heavy tails are landlord overshoot. Modeling's review does not mention it.
4. **The label-versus-proximity question has a within-building test after all.** Ads name neighbourhoods in their text, and brokers near a border claim the pricier one. An "ad claims a neighbourhood other than its building's area" flag varies within a building, so LOO can credit it, and it isolates the name's marketing value from location. It is a text feature Data can build in a day.
5. **Several descriptive terms have explanatory replacements worth testing:** the 68 bedroom-by-year noise scales (a noise scale driven by market velocity and by what the ad leaves unknown), the 2020–21 noise itself (the concession rows the cohort drops), and the per-building walk (permits plus neighbourhood curves). Each is one fit, and each trades a "this year was noisy" term for a reason.
6. **A strategy point for Data.** In these five neighbourhoods every building is within a short walk of several lines, parks and retail, so transit, parks and retail have little variance to price. The nulls are partly saturation. Explanatory location features will only be identifiable once the scrapes include contrast (poor transit, no retail, far from parks). Stuyvesant Town/PCV and the East Village are good for exactly that reason.

## 2. Thought experiment: what PSIS-LOO can see of a building-level feature

The served model has a building level `b_j ~ N(α + β·m̄_j, σ_b)` (within-building centring, research backlog E1 round 4). Under PSIS-LOO, row i of building j is predicted with `b_j` learnt from the building's other `n_j − 1` rows. A feature constant within the building moves only the prior mean of `b_j`. Its value for row i is the prior's weight in the posterior of `b_j`, which is `(1/σ_b²) / (1/σ_b² + (n_j − 1)/σ_row²)`.

With σ_b = 0.15, σ_row ≈ 0.095 (unit scale 0.086 plus noise), 2,941 buildings over 135k rows, and a quarter of the buildings at 5 rows or fewer (lognormal sizes), the expected ΔELPD of a building-level feature that explains a share of the building variance is (script: this thread's scratch `loo_power.py`):

| Share of building variance the feature explains | Expected ΔELPD, all rows | Of which, buildings with ≤5 rows | Fall in σ_b, in posterior SDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | +8 | +8 | 1.9 |
| 10% | +15 | +15 | 3.9 |
| 20% | +34 | +33 | 8.1 |
| 40% | +72 | +70 | 17 |

Reading it:
- Every location test to date sits at ±14 to ±17 SE. A feature has to explain about 20% of all building variance just to *expect* +2 SE under PSIS-LOO, with a coin-flip chance of actually clearing it, and about 40% to clear it reliably. Subway lines (+14.7, +20.5) and the garden side (+11.7) are consistent with explaining 10–20% of it.
- Almost all of the gain lives in buildings with 5 rows or fewer, which is where the location surface showed +16.8 ± 8.7. That partial result is the shape this calculation predicts.
- The same features are measured about four to eight times more precisely through σ_b. The variance decomposition already saves the posterior of every term, so the number is free.

The calculation is Gaussian and ignores the walk, the slopes and the Student-t tails, so it is a scale, not a forecast.

## 3. Amendments to Proposals A and B

**Explained share needs a null.** Any K building-level columns with a N(0, 0.5²) prior will absorb some building variance by chance: with 2,941 buildings, K columns take roughly K/2,941 of it, so the 250 m bump surface (about a hundred columns) can show a few percent "explained" that means nothing. Two cheap references:
- *Permutation:* the same column permuted across buildings, fitted once. One fit per feature family, not per feature.
- *Cross-validated screen:* in Proposal B, hold out buildings (grouped 5- or 10-fold) when regressing the levels on the candidate. The out-of-fold R² is the explained share without the optimism, and it is also the "new building" answer, because the held-out building's level was never used to fit the coefficient. That makes the 5 Modal fits of Proposal A.2 a confirmation for survivors, not a screen.

**Weight the screen properly.** Posterior means of small buildings' levels are shrunk toward the prior mean, so a plain regression under-states any feature that matters for small buildings. Use draws (regress each draw's levels, then average), or weight by `1 / (posterior SD² + residual variance)`.

**Report a "descriptive share" on every run.** The variance decomposition groups the neighbourhood labels under "features". Split them out and publish one number: descriptive share = neighbourhood labels + building level + building walk + unit level. The research goal is to drive that number down at equal PSIS-LOO. On the served fit it is about 9% building plus the neighbourhood and unit shares that the summary does not show separately (the handoff gives features 73.0%, building 8.9%, market and time 7.3%).

**A selection rule to put to Ben.** Among fits tied on PSIS-LOO within two combined SE, prefer the one with the lower descriptive share by more than its permutation null, *before* the elegance judges. That operationalises "explanatory over descriptive" as a rule rather than a case-by-case precedent. It is Ben's decision, as Modeling said.

## 4. New hypotheses and tests, ranked

Each entry gives the claim, why it is explanatory, how it is scored, and the cost. "LOO-creditable" means the feature varies within buildings, so PSIS-LOO can see it.

### 4.1 The final ask (data and likelihood; one fit)

*Claim.* Much of the heavy-tailed noise (ν ≈ 2) is landlord overshoot: the initial ask is a belief, the final ask is nearer the market. The cohort's target is the initial ask only (`cohort.PRICE_BASIS`).

*Test.* The served design on the same rows with the final ask as the target (from the listing extras' price-change record, which prevprice already read). Compare σ by bedroom group, ν, and the unit share. Scores on different targets do not pair, so the comparison is the noise model, not PSIS-LOO. Prediction: residual scales fall by 10% or more and ν rises.

*Then.* If it holds, Ben chooses the target. The scientific model of "what an apartment rents for" wants the final ask; the site's "what will this listing ask" wants the initial one, and a two-stage model (final ask, plus an overshoot that depends on management and market velocity) would serve both and give prevprice's information a valid, time-aware home.

*Cost.* One Modal fit once Data exposes `final_ask` on the frame (leak-free: it is the row's own ad, as `coded-v1` reads the row's own record).

### 4.2 The ad's claimed neighbourhood (text feature; LOO-creditable)

*Claim.* Ads for buildings near a border name the pricier neighbourhood. If the same building's ads that say "West Village" ask more than its ads that do not, the name has a marketing value separate from location.

*Build.* A regex over the ad text for the five area names plus their common aliases ("Village", "Chelsea", "Flatiron", "Gramercy", "NoMad" later). Columns: *claims own area*, *claims a different area* (which), *names none*. Reference: names none.

*Score.* PSIS-LOO, because it varies within a building; also the latest split, since the unit effect absorbs the unit's quality for repeat units and the remaining variation is the ad's wording.

*Confound.* Ads that claim the pricier name may be for better units or better brokers. Unit effects handle the first for repeat units; the second is itself part of "the name sells". Read the coefficient by distance to the border: a label effect concentrates there.

*Cost.* A day of Data work and one fit. Cheaper than the spatial-discontinuity pair, and complementary to it.

### 4.3 Explanatory noise instead of 68 scales (model term; one fit each)

The bedroom-by-year noise (+812) is the largest recent gain and the most descriptive term in the model: "3-bedroom asks in 2021 scattered more". Two replacements, each one mechanism:

- *Market velocity.* `log σ_t = a_g + c·|slope of the market trend at t|`: asks scatter when the market moves fast, because landlords disagree about where it is. One coefficient instead of 17 per bedroom group. 2020–22 and 2010–12 are the fast years.
- *What the ad leaves unknown.* `log σ_i = a_g + γ·(sqft unknown, floor unknown, description missing, unit listed once)`. Asks scatter more when the apartment is poorly described, and this is where the 5-bedroom and single-listing under-coverage sits.

Modeling's noise-by-neighbourhood is a third, descriptive option. I would run the two above first and keep the neighbourhood scales only if they still add on top.

### 4.4 Concessions and 2020–21 (data; a free check, then one fit)

*Claim.* The cohort drops ads that mention a concession (4,509 of 68k Chelsea rows in the September funnel). In 2020–21 concessions were the norm, so those years' trend and noise are estimated from the landlords who cut the face rent instead. Part of the "2020–21 noise" is this selection.

*Free check.* Excluded concession rows by year and by building class. If they concentrate in 2020–21 and in large elevator buildings, the trend there is biased up and the yearnoise gain is partly an artefact.

*Fit.* Bring them back with net-effective rent as the target where the months free are stated ("2 months free on a 14-month lease"), flagged. Score on shared rows, as a data rule.

### 4.5 Lease-ups, not heavier tails (free check, then a feature)

Single-listing units have tails twice as wide and 92% coverage. Modeling proposes heavier-tailed unit priors for new apartments. An explanatory alternative: single-listing rows cluster in new buildings' first months, where lease-up pricing (and undisclosed concessions) is unusual. Free check on the served residuals: split the single-listing tails by building size and by "months since the building's first listing in the data", and by `building era = 2010s+`. If the tails are a lease-up effect, the fix is a `lease_up` term (building's first 12 months × new construction), which is time-varying within the building and LOO-creditable, and the unit prior can stay.

### 4.6 Feature premiums that moved (model term; one fit)

The open "coefficient stability" item. The hedonic literature and the pandemic both say the premium for private outdoor space, in-unit laundry and a doorman changed around 2020. A test: three features × a post-2020 indicator (or × the bedroom-time knot basis for a smooth version). Explanatory ("the pandemic raised the price of a terrace"), creditable under LOO, and it competes directly with "pandemic shock by location" in Modeling's list, which I would run after it.

### 4.7 Replace the per-building walk (elegance test; one or two fits)

"Building over time" is 1% of the variance from 34 weakly identified steps per building. Once permits (renovation dates) and `area_time` (neighbourhood curves, already in `ModelConfig`) are in, test the design without the walk. If PSIS-LOO ties, the simpler model wins by rule, and the building's path over time has a reason (a permit, its neighbourhood) rather than a path.

### 4.8 Light and view (unit level; agrees with Modeling)

Build from the footprints snapshot: for each unit's floor and facing side, the height of the nearest building across the street or lot line, and whether the unit's floor is above it. Prediction: it takes variance from the per-building floor slope and from the unit level.

### 4.9 Lower priority, noted for completeness

- *Zoned schools × 2BR+* (Modeling's #2): fine, but note that in these neighbourhoods the zones are PS 3, 11, 33, 40, 41 and 116, all well regarded, so variance is small until the scrapes widen.
- *HowLoud scores:* a current snapshot would carry later information; the 311-based `noise_v1` is dated and should be tried first.
- *Stabilization:* null on all rows as expected for a slow building-level share; the ad-level `rent_stabilized` flag is the right carrier and is already in `desc-v1`.
- *Lines and parks:* judge by explained share after the screen, as Modeling says; expect little until there is geographic contrast.
