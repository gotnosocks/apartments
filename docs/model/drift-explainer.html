The fits behind this page

What it took to trust the building's path

The first fits with a path for each building predicted better than the model without one and missed the convergence gate on one number, the spread of building levels outside Manhattan (a split R-hat of 1.013 to 1.045 across three fits, where the gate allows 1.01). For its first hours, on November 10, the Deals list rested on the model without that path.

The best-supported cause is about ten buildings in 68,000. In the eight that were read, some ads are listed as studios with no description at the rent of the building's three-bedrooms. Under a bell-curve prior each such building pulls on the spread that every building shares, and chains that have settled those ten differently disagree about the spread.

A prior with heavier tails (a Student-t with 4 degrees of freedom) lets a building sit far from its neighbourhood without moving the rest. With it all three full fits pass the gate, that spread's R-hat is 1.005 or lower in each, and the held-out score rose a little. The list rests on this model now.

What the score cannot tell you

  • Why an ask is low. A landlord priced low, the apartment has a flaw the ad does not state, or the ad is wrong. The model sees all three as the same low ask.
  • What it rented for. The data holds asking rents, net of any free months. An ad that left the market may have rented for less.
  • Apartments it has never seen. A unit with no earlier ad is priced from its features, its building and its neighbourhood. Its range is wider, so a low ask there is weaker evidence.
  • The backtest is not fully blind. An ad's feature list comes from its latest record, which can be newer than the backtest's day. No price crosses that line.
