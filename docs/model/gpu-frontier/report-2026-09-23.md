## Summary

- **Best score so far: `m4-walk-bedtime-bedslope` (commit `942c0f4`).** It adds three
  pieces to the base model: a per-building time walk, bedroom-group market curves and a
  per-building bedroom slope. Row-split **ΔELPD +271.5 ± 36.5** against the promoted model on
  the same 5,264 held-out rows. Unit-split ELPD is 4,328.7 (m0 base: 3,540.7), with the
  promoted model's unit-split reference still pending. It is not yet eligible as *current
  best*: its slowest scale parameter has split R-hat 1.019–1.023 against the 1.01 gate,
  with bulk ESS ≥ 1,380.
- **Current best (passes all checks): `m0-base` (commit `5cc0809`).** Row-split ΔELPD
  −827 ± 50, 83 s and $0.18 per fit on one H100. It is also the only frontier entry.
- **Inference.** A structured blocked Gibbs sampler in JAX draws every latent effect
  jointly and exactly each iteration: coefficients, month trend, season, each building's
  local block and each unit. The group scales get a collapsed Metropolis update with all
  latents integrated out, computed from the same block factorisation. Its correctness is
  tested:
  - the joint draw matches a dense solve to 1e-8;
  - the collapsed marginal likelihood matches a dense multivariate normal;
  - posterior moments match NumPyro NUTS on synthetic data.
- **Interpretability.** `explain.py` gives each apartment its fitted (median) rent and its
  named dollar contributions, with 90% intervals: market, bedrooms, bathrooms, size, floor,
  amenity groups, building, building time drift, building bedroom premium and unit. It
  also gives the residual. The contributions add up exactly to the fitted rent (logarithmic-mean
  decomposition, checked on every draw).

## Why the leading designs fail the gate, and what is left

The failure is sampler efficiency, not disagreement between chains. For every parameter,
observed split R-hat equals the value expected from an unbiased sampler at that
parameter's ESS per chain. The binding parameter is the building walk scale, at about
0.035 effective draws per iteration:

| run | chains × draws | walk-scale ESS | max R-hat |
|---|---|---|---|
| m1 @ 94a4da4 (plain Gibbs) | 32 × 1000 | 552 | 1.054 |
| m1 @ 1b11fac (+ rescaling move) | 32 × 1000 | 648 | 1.044 |
| m1 @ 942c0f4 (+ collapsed scales) | 32 × 1000 | 1,786 | 1.024 (σ) |
| m1 @ c5a208a (+ σ collapsed, adaptive steps) | 16 × 2000 | 1,114 | 1.020 |

Held-out ELPD is stable to ±0.5 across all four m1 runs (+70.2 to +70.5), so the ranking
of designs is not in doubt. The gate is about interval-grade convergence.

Next fix: a one-dimensional collapsed update for the walk scale, and possibly for σ, with
its own proposal size, in addition to the joint step. Alternatively, the cheaper one of:
(a) run about 3× more draws per chain; (b) replace the half-normal walk-scale prior's
Metropolis step with slice sampling on the collapsed conditional.

## Tried and ruled out

- BlackJAX many-chain HMC (ChEES, MEADS) on the same NumPyro model. Gradients are
  50–170× cheaper per chain on the GPU, but no configuration mixed (R-hat 8–16). Causes:
  degenerate joint-mode initialisation, trajectory collapse under dispersed starts, and
  ADVI failing on the centered model. Code kept in `sample.py`, commit `7561cab`.
- Laplace or INLA via BlackJAX or pymc-extras: both need a dense Hessian (4–31 GB).
